## A. 2019 to 2021: design and first runs of today's aggregation

### F1. The lottery is private as protection against DoS; the public alternative was known two epochs ahead; the price is a chance of no aggregator

- **Source.** `pos-consensus`, 2020-02-24, sproul, [`pos-consensus/2020-02-24.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/pos-consensus/2020-02-24.json) #0. `apis`, 2020-07-04, protolambda, [`apis/2020-07-04.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/apis/2020-07-04.json) #6.
- **Quote.** “The advantage of the current scheme seems to be that nobody knows the aggregators in advance (until the aggregator broadcasts their aggregate), and can't try to DoS them? Whereas in the shuffling-based approach, they would be known 2 epochs in advance, and vulnerable for that period”. And: “This way, we get TARGET_AGGREGATORS_PER_COMMITTEE secretly selected aggregators, who can proof their role with their signature. On average.”
- **Bears on.** DoS resilience, the two regimes: supports. Sharpens two points. The secrecy lasts only “until the aggregator broadcasts their aggregate”. And a private lottery gives an expected count, so the baseline's k = 16 is a mean, and a committee with no aggregator is a loss with no adversary.
- **Numbers.** No aggregator in a committee: “something like once every 100 days for high validator counts” (sproul, from memory). sproul's question got no reply in the archive. Inference (mine): at n = 390 and probability 1/24, (23/24)^390 ≈ 6.2 · 10⁻⁸ per committee and slot, which is one empty committee in about 35 days over 64 committees and 7,200 slots a day.

### F2. Why 16: the one numeric argument in the archive is about the lottery's variance

- **Source.** `specifications`, 2021-07-08 to 2021-07-12, jgm and hsiaowei, [`specifications/2021-07-08.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/specifications/2021-07-08.json) #12, [`specifications/2021-07-09.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/specifications/2021-07-09.json) #11, [`specifications/2021-07-12.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/specifications/2021-07-12.json) #0 and #1. The subject is the sync committee's aggregators.
- **Quote.** “128 validators means that there is roughly a 1.7% `(1-0.03125)^128` chance that there are 0 aggregators for a given subnet for a slot”. Then: “If `TARGET_AGGREGATORS_PER_SYNC_SUBCOMMITTEE` were increased to 16 that would pretty much solve the problem, but obviously then we're at a 1 in 8 chance of a sync committee member being an aggregator so a fair bit more overhead.” And: “8 gives basically no chance, 16 gives a very high chance).”
- **Bears on.** Baseline (k = 16): supports the number and sharpens its reason. The reason given is that a lottery with a small target leaves subnets empty, not Byzantine or DoS tolerance. A draw of exactly k aggregators does not need 16 for this reason. The same figures came back in 2026 for 4 PQ aggregators (F23).
- **Numbers.** Target 4 of 128: 1.7 % empty per subnet and slot, 93 % of slots with all 4 subnets served, 10.8 % of epochs. Target 8: 96 % of epochs, but over a day (225 epochs) “pretty much guaranteed that there will be at least one subcommittee without aggregators”. Inference (mine): a clean day has probability 0.06 % at target 8 and 99.9 % at target 16. The archive does not show the decision that followed.

### F3. Structured aggregation was set aside in 2019 because a pre-determined structure gives up validator privacy

- **Source.** `consensus-dev`, 2019-10-24, .fjl, [`consensus-dev/2019-10-24.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/consensus-dev/2019-10-24.json) #33. `consensus-dev`, 2019-10-25, protolambda via bridge, [`consensus-dev/2019-10-25.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/consensus-dev/2019-10-25.json) #0.
- **Quote.** “another question: doesn't any kind of pre-determined network structure throw out the idea of validator privacy?” Answer: “Yes it does fjl, which is why I'm giving handel-like tree heuristics on gossipsub a try.” and “And otherwise we have to research more structured but privacy-iffy approaches like Handel”.
- **Bears on.** DoS resilience with known aggregators, and Anonymity enhancing: sharpens. Layers, grids and any dispersed assignment are a pre-determined structure in this sense. The designers of today's stack counted that as a loss of privacy in itself, and it is the stated reason why today's aggregation has no structure.
- **Numbers.** None.

### F4. Dropping small aggregates for bigger ones was named a liveness hazard in 2019

- **Source.** `consensus-dev`, 2019-10-25, protolambda via bridge, [`consensus-dev/2019-10-25.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/consensus-dev/2019-10-25.json) #3. `consensus-dev`, 2019-10-05, djrtwo, [`consensus-dev/2019-10-05.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/consensus-dev/2019-10-05.json) #10.
- **Quote.** “Or you start optimistically dropping (i.e. drop small ones without seeing the bigger aggregate), but you get in a very gray liveness problem area.” And: “Its important to note that a strategy in which all participants aggregate and rebroadcast the small aggregates as that go, will result in disorder -- tons of non-aggregable small attestations.”
- **Bears on.** No displacement: supports. Merge and Redundant work: sharpens. With BLS, aggregates that overlap cannot be combined, which is why aggregation became one shot at the end of the slot by a few aggregators. Proofs that may overlap (F22) remove that reason; proving time replaces it.
- **Numbers.** None.

### F5. Single votes only on subnets, and aggregates only from aggregators, as a bound on the number of distinct messages

- **Source.** `pos-consensus`, 2020-08-10, ajsutton, [`pos-consensus/2020-08-10.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/pos-consensus/2020-08-10.json) #1 and #3.
- **Quote.** “Otherwise a node could aggregate and broadcast a huge number of different attestations to swamp the network. Whereas with these rules there’s a much more limited number of unique messages that can be sent and they have to come from the actual validator not just someone who collected and aggregated them in different ways.”
- **Bears on.** Sybil resilience: supports, and sharpens it. The bound has two parts: who may send, and how many distinct valid messages one sender can make. A proof over any subset of the votes is a distinct valid message, so a small sender set is not enough without a budget per sender (F18, F26).
- **Numbers.** None.

### F6. Aggregating was never paid; it was justified by self-interest in one's own vote, and operators skipped redundant aggregates from the start

- **Source.** `pos-consensus`, 2020-04-07, ajsutton, [`pos-consensus/2020-04-07.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/pos-consensus/2020-04-07.json) #0. `consensus-dev`, 2020-12-06, jgm and benjaminion, [`consensus-dev/2020-12-06.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/consensus-dev/2020-12-06.json) #1 and #2. `consensus-dev`, 2024-04-21, ajsutton, [`consensus-dev/2024-04-21.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/consensus-dev/2024-04-21.json) #4.
- **Quote.** “I don't believe there's any reward for aggregating so I'm inclined to only publish one of them.” Then: “No, aggregating is a common good.” And: “there is an _implicit_ reward for me as a validator to produce good aggregations: if I want _my_ attestation to be included, I should try to include it in a juicy aggregate to make it attractive for the proposer to include.” In 2024: “We sometimes define honest behaviour as doing something altruistic (or very indirectly incentivised) but generally try to avoid specking honest behaviour that is obviously against self-interest.”
- **Bears on.** Incentive compatibility: supports the baseline row. Sharpens it: the implicit reward works while the duty costs next to nothing. Under PQ the duty costs seconds of every core (F28), and the same absence of reward is carried over (F31).
- **Numbers.** None.

### F7. Gossip suppresses an aggregate already seen; a node in 2025 received about 6 aggregates per committee, 4 of them distinct, not 16

- **Source.** `pos-consensus`, 2020-04-07, paulhauner, [`pos-consensus/2020-04-07.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/pos-consensus/2020-04-07.json) #1. `consensus-dev`, 2025-12-19, potuz, m.kalinin, raulvk, [`consensus-dev/2025-12-19.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/consensus-dev/2025-12-19.json) #3, #9, #11, #22. `consensus-dev`, 2025-12-20, nflaig, [`consensus-dev/2025-12-20.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/consensus-dev/2025-12-20.json) #0. `pos-consensus`, 2020-10-31, barnabemonnot, [`pos-consensus/2020-10-31.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/pos-consensus/2020-10-31.json) #4.
- **Quote.** 2020: “the gossip network would try to supress the second `AggregateAndProof` since its `aggregate` has already been seen.” 2025: “I was surprised to see several hundreds of identical aggregates coming in per slot, only difference is aggregator index.” with the log “aggregateAttAndProofCount=377” and “uniqueAggregateAttAndProofCount=267”, and the reply “Average numbers are 5.84 and 4.17 respectively. Avg nums doesn't look unhealthy to me”.
- **Bears on.** Redundant work, and the baseline's "about 1,024 aggregators per slot": sharpens. 1,024 counts producers. What a node receives and checks is a few hundred, of which about 30 % are copies that differ only in the aggregator's signature: “If the aggregates are signed then it’s semantically identical aggregations will still be seen as unique by gossipsub and forwarded/gossiped if validation passes (since the message id func is a hash of the payload)”.
- **Numbers.** 377 received and 267 distinct in one slot over 64 committees: 5.9 and 4.2 per committee. It is one log line from a Prysm node that was catching up, and Lodestar reported a bug that let copies through, so take it as an order of magnitude. Medalla, 2020: “~350M unique votes contained in ~14M aggregates on chain, hence the 23x ratio”.

### F8. Validating nodes could be identified by their subnet subscriptions; random long-lived subscriptions were the answer, and “not really a foolproof way”

- **Source.** `networking`, 2020-08-05, p_bitfly and nishant0, [`networking/2020-08-05.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/networking/2020-08-05.json) #0 to #2. `consensus-dev`, 2021-06-17, protolambda, [`consensus-dev/2021-06-17.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/consensus-dev/2021-06-17.json) #4 and #8. `networking`, 2022-08-17, parithosh, [`networking/2022-08-17.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/networking/2022-08-17.json) #5.
- **Quote.** “currently it is very easy to identify validating nodes by their attestation subnetwork subscriptions that are advertized via the p2p protocol. this makes them an easy dos target”. Reply, on the random subnets: “not really a foolproof way, but it does help”. 2021: “Validator privacy is the main line of defense for smaller stakers.” 2022: “by default it is possible to link IPs to validator public keys if you listen to gossip long enough and deploy a wide enough network of listeners”.
- **Bears on.** Network adversary, "what it knows": supports taking the map from validators to machines as given. DoS resilience with secret aggregators, "as long as nothing leaks": sharpens, since the leak was called easy in 2020 and possible by default in 2022. Anonymity enhancing: supports.
- **Numbers.** None. The archive has nothing on an aggregator being revealed by joining its subnet (see the last list).

### F9. First testnets: with few validators the lottery chose almost nobody, and a missing aggregate meant the votes were lost although nodes held them

- **Source.** `witti`, 2020-05-27, paulhauner, [`witti/2020-05-27.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/witti/2020-05-27.json) #107 and #110.
- **Quote.** “I'm not seeing many aggregates.. last epoch I only observed 8 aggregators, the epoch before that only 1 aggregator”. And: “If we don't get an aggregate then we don't attempt to produce one from naive aggregation pool”.
- **Bears on.** Recoverable, fallback: supports. A node that holds the votes and may not, or does not, aggregate them is the failure that a fallback removes.
- **Numbers.** 30 active validators under mainnet constants.

### F10. Mainnet at genesis: aggregation worked very well; a vote that missed the aggregates depended on being picked up alone within 31 slots

- **Source.** `consensus-dev`, 2020-12-05, jgm, [`consensus-dev/2020-12-05.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/consensus-dev/2020-12-05.json) #16. `pyrmont`, 2020-12-31, djrtwo, [`pyrmont/2020-12-31.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/pyrmont/2020-12-31.json) #33. `pos-consensus`, 2020-04-24, djrtwo, [`pos-consensus/2020-04-24.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/pos-consensus/2020-04-24.json) #2.
- **Quote.** “mainnet attestation aggregation is being _ludicrously_ effective.  Obviously helped by the high participation, but even then number of blocks that come through with <10 attestations is impressive.” And: “if you don't make it into an aggregate, then you are hoping that you get picked up in someones pool as a single sometime in the next 31 slots”. On why aggregates go to everyone: “A proposer might miss their block or not optimally include attestations, so it's valuable that proposers of subsequent slots also have the aggregates on hand”.
- **Bears on.** Complete: supports for the happy case. Recoverable, late inclusion: supports; today's stack has a window of 32 slots and proposers that add single votes. Delivered, level 2: the reason given for sending aggregates to every node is the next proposers, and fork choice.
- **Numbers.** Fewer than 10 attestations in a block; 31 slots.

### F11. Nodes on the aggregate topic that do not forward were suspected of costing votes on Medalla

- **Source.** `networking`, 2020-08-24, agemanning, [`networking/2020-08-24.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/networking/2020-08-24.json) #3.
- **Quote.** “One reason for losing attestations could be that we have enough clients syncing on the aggregate attestation channel and dropping the aggregates such that they don't all propagate to listening peers (i think we'd need a good % of censoring nodes, but could be a contributing factor)”.
- **Bears on.** Names something our list lacks: relays that do not forward. Our network adversary silences machines and cannot touch messages between the others; a node that sits on a topic and drops what it gets is neither. It belongs to Reliable, or to no displacement on the propagation side.
- **Numbers.** None.

## B. 2022 to 2025: how today's stack behaves

### F12. Mainnet timing in 2023: 94 % of votes by 6 s; nearly all aggregates between 8 s and 10 s

- **Source.** `consensus-dev`, 2023-06-15, arnetheduck, [`consensus-dev/2023-06-15.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/consensus-dev/2023-06-15.json) #0 and #2.
- **Quote.** “it's _generally_ enough that one of 16 aggregators send out a good aggregate in time for block production”. From the histogram of one Nimbus node: “beacon_aggregate_delay_bucket{le="8.0"} 72015.0” and “beacon_aggregate_delay_bucket{le="10.0"} 23319966.0”.
- **Bears on.** Latency: sharpens the baseline. Aggregates of a few hundred bytes take up to 2 s to reach a node after they are published at 8 s. Q19's aggregation phase of 2 s is that long before any proving.
- **Numbers.** Votes received by 4 s: 35.9 %; by 6 s: 94.0 %; by 8 s: 99.5 %. Aggregates by 8 s: 0.3 %; by 10 s: 99.34 %; by 12 s: 99.74 % (23.5 million aggregates, percentages computed from the buckets).

### F13. BLS aggregation took seconds in one client, and its cutoff for votes sat 1.5 s before the publishing time

- **Source.** `consensus-dev`, 2023-05-31, potuz and m.kalinin, [`consensus-dev/2023-05-31.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/consensus-dev/2023-05-31.json) #0 and #1. `consensus-dev`, 2023-06-15, potuz, [`consensus-dev/2023-06-15.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/consensus-dev/2023-06-15.json) #15 and #22.
- **Quote.** “Aggregation takes up to 3-4 seconds on lower end hardware and up to 2 seconds on the usual dedicated hardware (eg a NUC)”. And: “At prysm we computed that we need to have at the very least 1.5 seconds before the 8 seconds mark to be sure to have a good aggregate in the 99% of cases”. On the deadline: “If >40% of aggregates are late then they can reorg a block produced in the next slot right after its proposal.”
- **Bears on.** Timely vote: sharpens. The cutoff is each aggregator's own and already differs between clients (6.5 s against 8 s). The baseline row "Bandwidth and CPU load: not the constraint": contradicted in part, since CPU bound one client in 2023. The hard deadline comes from fork choice, which decoupled consensus does not have.
- **Numbers.** 2 to 4 s; 1.5 s; 40 %.

### F14. With 16 aggregators and no attack, votes are lost at the hand-over to the aggregators; the requirement is stated per vote

- **Source.** `pq-networking`, 2026-11-12, potuz, [`pq-networking/2026-11-12.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/pq-networking/2026-11-12.json) #9, #10, #13. `pq-cl`, thread "Post-Quantum Consensus Signatures EIP", 2026-11-10, potuz, [`pq-cl/_threads/Post-Quantum Consensus Signatures EIP/2026-11-10.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/pq-cl/_threads/Post-Quantum%20Consensus%20Signatures%20EIP/2026-11-10.json) #4 and #9. `interop`, 2025-02-28, nflaig, [`interop-🌃/2025-02-28.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/interop-%F0%9F%8C%83/2025-02-28.json) #35. `allcoredevs`, 2023-02-28, potuz, [`allcoredevs/2023-02-28.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/allcoredevs/2023-02-28.json) #6.
- **Quote.** “Yes the main reason for missed head votes today is not being included in aggregates”. And: “I just took a bunch of nodes and verified that they were peered and their attestations were sent in time but they are never included or included later in a very poor aggregate (either low bit list or mostly covered before)”. The requirement: “What you want is not too guarantee that there's one aggregate, you want a guarantee that every single attestation will reach an aggregate”. In 2025: “Lodestar missed attestations are primarily due to no aggregate inclusion”.
- **Bears on.** Complete and Timely vote: contradicts the comfort of our split. We call a vote timely when it reached every honest aggregator, and owe it nothing otherwise. Practitioners place the loss exactly there. Names something our list lacks: k as reach. The number of aggregators is also the number of places a vote can arrive at in time, whatever the faults: “At 4 per subnet it'll be much harder to find them”.
- **Numbers.** “say you have 50 peers and 2 validators. You need 2 agregators . Out of your 50 you will have less than 7 aggregators”.

### F15. Aggregators or proposers on all subnets were refused as a requirement for home stakers, and nodes on all subnets failed first on a testnet of 2.1 million validators

- **Source.** `interop`, 2023-08-10, nishant0, [`interop-🌃/2023-08-10.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/interop-%F0%9F%8C%83/2023-08-10.json) #22. `networking`, 2024-03-31 and 2024-04-01, potuz, [`networking/2024-03-31.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/networking/2024-03-31.json) #0 and [`networking/2024-04-01.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/networking/2024-04-01.json) #1. `consensus-dev`, 2024-01-25, jgm, [`consensus-dev/2024-01-25.json`](https://github.com/ethereum/eth-rnd-archive/blob/18a6ec37623deb1b682080bedb3bddc854a95d0c/consensus-dev/2024-01-25.json) #44.
- **Quote.** “I suspect gossip queues are overloaded due to each node subscribing to all the subnets. So you have a node's attestations being dropped before it can be broadcasted out to the network.” On EIP-7549: “will aggregators be required to register to all subnets? cause I suspect that's a departure from current hardware requirement for home stakers”. On a proposer that aggregates: “the proposer would need to be on all subnets and smaller stakers may be swamped.”
- **Bears on.** "An aggregator joins every subnet it covers" and Low load per node: sharpens. ## Requirements and properties stated there that our list lacks

- **Reach.** Every vote reaches an aggregator, stated per vote and with everyone honest (F14, F18). The number of aggregators counts as places to arrive at, not only as spares.
- **Variance of the selection.** The probability that a subnet has no aggregator, or one, under a lottery (F1, F2, F23).
- **A mixed regime.** Some aggregators private and some public (F23).
- **Messages per sender.** A budget of distinct messages per aggregator and slot, beside the size of the sender set (F5, F17, F26).
- **Mass offline.** The votes of those still online keep being included when most validators are offline (F20).
- **Missed proposals.** Tolerance counted in slots of backlog; 80 % of blocks missing was named (F32).
- **Relays that do not forward.** Nodes on a topic that drop what they receive (F11).

## Where a statement contradicts our list

- **How the `pq` spec draws aggregators.** Described in November as a lottery with a target of 4 over a per-validator secret, against our "4 public aggregators per committee" and "a private PQ lottery needs a PQ VRF" (F23, F33). A later message suggests positioned aggregators. To settle in the spec.
- **No displacement in the `pq` gossip.** Within the slot the heavier aggregate wins; the superset rule is for late aggregates (F26). A silent-if-close variant of staggering is being pushed (F27).
- **Latency floors.** One merge takes 2.2 to 4 s, and 4.5 s next to an execution client, on the hardware called the upper end of solo staking; our floors of 1.61 to 3.14 s through one merge are below that on such machines (F28).
- **"One honest aggregator is enough" as a target.** The author of F18 would not go as far as 1 good aggregator of 16.
- **Complete.** Today's stack with 16 aggregators loses timely-sent votes at the hand-over, and devnet-0 lost 9 points with no adversary (F14, F34).
- **The baseline's 1,024.** It counts producers; a node receives a few hundred aggregates per slot (F7).
- **The baseline's "CPU load: not the constraint".** BLS aggregation took 2 to 4 s in one client in 2023 (F13).
- **N = 250 from 8,000 nodes** in the attestation file, against about 400 to 500 from 13,000 nodes (F19).
- **One slot of silence costs one slot of roles.** Not under top-layer caching (F39).
- **Blobs as a spare carrier.** Proofs and columns compete for the same uplink (F37).
