# Attestation broadcast

**What it is.** The first stage of a finality vote's path. A validator signs a vote, and the signature reaches the nodes on its subnet, among them the aggregators that cover that subnet. [Aggregation broadcast](aggregation-broadcast.md) takes over from there.

## Terms

- **Phase.** The window in which one group of validators votes: a slot in today's pipeline, a 4 s + 4 s unit in [Q17](../research/fg/pipelined-units/README.md). v is the number of votes in a phase.
- **Subnet.** n validators that vote in the same phase and gossip their signatures on one topic. A phase has S subnets, so n = v / S.
  - `external` Reference spec: 16 subnets, and a validator's subnet never changes: "A validator's committee index is fixed by its validator index, and so is its subnet" ([`validator.md` L280–L283](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/validator.md?plain=1#L280-L283), [`p2p-interface.md` L65–L71](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/p2p-interface.md?plain=1#L65-L71)). A slot has 256 committees, 16 on each subnet ([`beacon-chain.md` L721–L730](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/beacon-chain.md?plain=1#L721-L730)). At 800,000 validators v is about 100,000 votes per slot and n about 6,250, four times a subnet of the `pq` spec (the agent's arithmetic). Not upstream as far as the fixed subnet goes: there the committees are an even split of the active validators "in index order rotated by `epoch`" ([upstream `beacon-chain.md` L1962–L1964](https://github.com/ethereum/consensus-specs/blob/bde2349e1e86763aed845e8a064cb22805628e0c/specs/_features/pq/beacon-chain.md?plain=1#L1962-L1964)), so a validator changes committee and subnet about every 390 epochs at 800,000 validators (the agent's arithmetic). The counts and sizes are the same.
- **Nodes per subnet N.** With P nodes that each join j subnets, N = P·j / S. Nodes that join a subnet for a duty come on top of those. Today N is about 400 to 500, with default nodes on 2 of the 64 subnets (Yann, 2026-11-25). It is not the number of validators that vote on the subnet: one node can run many.
  - `external` The figure has a derivation: 13,000 nodes on 2 of 64 subnets give about 400 mesh members, "plus aggregators + observers, maybe 500?" ([discord F19](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). In 2023, with 4,200 nodes measured and before nodes moved to 2 of 64, a subnet also had about 500, and the change was expected to bring that under 200 ([specs 10](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)).
  - `external` Nothing sets a floor under N, and nothing pays for backing a subnet: "the rational behavior is to turn your attnets down to 1 or even 0" ([specs 10](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). Asked of a backbone in 2021: subnets that are stable from slot to slot, entry points found in under a minute, and if possible a way to tell whether a node does its duty ([specs 10](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). Splitting committees over more subnets was held back by the same number: "without sufficient nodes on the network, you might either reduce the number of nodes per subnet to dangerous levels" ([specs 9](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)).

The number of subnets is not an input here. The [aggregation strategy](../research/pq-aggregation/README.md) sets it.

## Properties

Right after its name, every property carries a tag that says where it comes from ([legend](README.md#origin-tags)). A tag covers what the property asks for; the details under it are an agent's unless they say otherwise.

- **Timely.** `human` (P1 of Yann's design in Q17) A vote sent on time reaches the aggregators of its subnet before they cut off.
  - Every voter keeps the propagation budget it has today, wherever it is: "at least 4 s before aggregation" (P1 in [Q17](../research/fg/pipelined-units/README.md)).
  - Open under PQ: "it doesn't seem to me that we can disseminate ~1'600 PQ attestations per subnet in ~4 seconds, if we keep everything else equal" ([PQ Gasper avenues](../posts/pq-gasper-avenues.md)).
  - `external` Measured today, with BLS votes of a few hundred bytes: in slots without a block, votes arrive on average 831 ms after the vote deadline, a subnet's supermajority forms in under 830 ms, and the 95th percentile takes 1,962 ms ([ethresear.ch 28](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). On one node 94 % of the votes had arrived by 6 s of the slot ([discord F12](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).
  - `external` The cutoff is each aggregator's own: its publishing time minus its proving time ([discord F21](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). One client needed the votes 1.5 s before the publishing time with BLS ([discord F13](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)), and the `pq` spec staggers four cutoffs per committee ([specs 23](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)).
  - `external` Reaching an aggregator is where votes are lost today: "the main reason for missed head votes today is not being included in aggregates". What is asked for is "a guarantee that every single attestation will reach an aggregate" ([discord F14](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). [Reach](aggregation-broadcast.md#reach) states it on the other line.
  - `external` Under PQ: one subnet's votes need 8.85 to 17.69 Mbit/s over the `pq` spec's four cutoffs, at one copy per vote ([ethresear.ch 10](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). The first PQ devnet saw 86 % of the votes on gossip, with many single votes arriving after the 6 s deadline ([discord F34](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).
  - `external` Publishing a vote does not wait for anything else. The Simplex pyspec says it of the available committee's topic: "Aggregation MUST NOT delay this initial publication" ([specs 35](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)).
  - `external` Reference spec: a validator makes its vote "at or after the support cutoff", half way through its slot. Its deadline is a TODO, and so is everything about aggregators, their cutoff included ([`validator.md` L709–L719](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/validator.md?plain=1#L709-L719), [`validator.md` L933–L936](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/validator.md?plain=1#L933-L936)).
- **Low load per node.** `human` (Yann's requirement) Tracked separately for validators and other nodes ([requirements](../design/requirements.md#load)), against EIP-7870's budget ([Q20](../research/open_questions.md)).
  - Per node: subnets joined × signatures per subnet × (signature + overhead) × copies (Barnabé's model, in [`research/pq/`](../research/pq/README.md)).
  - `external` The signature size is still moving: 3,112 bytes in June 2026, 2,404 to 2,548 bytes per vote in November's simulations and estimates, and 1,204 if peers cache a signature's top layer ([ethresear.ch 16](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md), [discord F25](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md), [discord F39](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). The threshold its designers aim at is one packet: "if we can get signature sizes below one MTU" ([ethresear.ch 16](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)).
  - `external` Simulations of November 2026 agree that the lowest tier cannot be on a vote subnet as things stand: "the lowest tier validator node (50/25 mbps) doesn't aggregate and doesn't subscribe to attestation subnet", and an attester on one subnet sends up to 114 Mbit/s against EIP-7870's 25. Copies were about 9 per vote in a simulation of 1,000 nodes ([discord F25](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).
  - `external` Nodes on every subnet fail first: on a testnet of 2.1 million validators their gossip queues were suspected of overflowing, so that a node's own votes were dropped before they went out ([discord F15](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).
- **Fair vote timing.** `human` No validator is forced to send at a bad time ([properties](README.md)).
  - `external` Voters time their votes too: "rational attesters may delay their attestation deadline to vote accurately" ([ethresear.ch 29](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md), [specs 14](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). A finality vote in DC has no head to get right, so there is less to gain from waiting (the research note's inference). Clients that sign slowly lose most of their votes at the cutoff ([discord F34](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).
  - `external` Reference spec: the send time rotates. The committees are cut into one block per slot of a round, "neighbouring blocks swap slots" from one round to the next, and a committee "votes in every slot twice every `2 * SLOTS_PER_ROUND` rounds" ([`beacon-chain.md` L1579–L1586](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/beacon-chain.md?plain=1#L1579-L1586)). Not upstream: there a committee always votes in the same slot of the round, "The committees of a round are assigned to its slots in order" ([upstream `beacon-chain.md` L1402](https://github.com/ethereum/consensus-specs/blob/bde2349e1e86763aed845e8a064cb22805628e0c/specs/_features/pq/beacon-chain.md?plain=1#L1402)). A validator's slot changes only as it drifts through the committees, about every 100,000 epochs at 800,000 validators (the agent's arithmetic).
- **Reliable.** `human` Votes still arrive when the network is slow, lossy or partly partitioned ([properties](README.md)).
  - `external` Two adversaries of this stage silence nobody. One joins a subnet with many nodes: node IDs decide who backs which subnet, so "An attack on a single subnet would require just 1/64 of malicious nodes than attacking the whole network" ([specs 11](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). The other sits on a topic and does not forward what it receives ([discord F11](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).
- **Anonymity enhancing.** `human` A node's own votes look like relaying, and validators do not move across subnets ([properties](README.md)).
  - `external` What leaks today is the subscription: "currently it is very easy to identify validating nodes by their attestation subnetwork subscriptions" ([discord F8](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)), and the aggregator is the attester that has to subscribe ([specs 12](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md), [ethresear.ch 31](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). Protection per message is not enough, because the same validator sends again and again ([ethresear.ch 35](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)).
  - `external` Anonymity at the network layer was judged infeasible for the consensus layer "because of latency constraints", unless changes at the consensus layer relax them ([ethresear.ch 35](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). Finality votes in DC are such a change (the research note's inference).
  - `external` Reference spec: validators do not move across subnets, because committee and subnet follow from the validator index ([`validator.md` L280–L283](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/validator.md?plain=1#L280-L283)). The other side of a fixed assignment: anyone can compute on which subnet, and in which slot of a round, a given validator sends (the agent's reading). Not upstream: there a validator moves to the next subnet about every 390 epochs at 800,000 validators ([Terms](#terms)). That rule is public too, so anyone can compute it there as well.
- **Bounded spam.** `agent` A node forwards a bounded number of votes per validator, after cheap checks. The `pq` spec at `ee867e9` accepts one per target epoch and validator, on the right subnet, from a committee member, with a valid signature ([design note](../research/pq-gasper/design.md)).
  - `external` One vote per validator may be one too few. The specs for decoupled consensus forward a second, different vote from the same validator, so that an equivocation can be detected over gossip: "One second distinct message is also forwarded and marks the validator as a known round equivocator" ([specs 32](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md): the Simplex pyspec, for finality votes; an open pull request on the EIP-8453 branch does the same for head votes, two per validator and slot). The bound is then two per validator and round.
  - `external` The order of the checks matters. Tying a vote to a staked signer before any work that depends on the state "closes a loophole where clients either must drop attestations or risk being overwhelmed by shuffling computations" ([specs 20](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)).
  - `external` Valid votes can overload as well: in May 2023 votes with old targets made two clients rebuild old states again and again ([ethresear.ch 19](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)).
  - `external` Under leanXMSS a vote uses up a leaf of its key: "a validator attests once per round and each attestation needs its own XMSS leaf" ([specs 28](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)).
  - `external` Reference spec: gossip forwards one FG attestation per validator and round for the current target, and one per validator for the previous target. "Only attestations for the current target of the head state, and attestations for its previous target whose finality pair names a pending justification of the head state, are propagated, as only those can be included with an effect" ([`p2p-interface.md` L1031–L1040](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/p2p-interface.md?plain=1#L1031-L1040)). The signature uses the leanXMSS leaf of the round, so "a validator MUST NOT sign two different attestations in the same round" ([`validator.md` L893–L896](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/validator.md?plain=1#L893-L896)). Not upstream as far as the previous target goes: there gossip has the first rule only, "No other valid attestation seen for this round and validator" ([upstream `p2p-interface.md` L1004](https://github.com/ethereum/consensus-specs/blob/bde2349e1e86763aed845e8a064cb22805628e0c/specs/_features/pq/p2p-interface.md?plain=1#L1004)), and does not ask that the target be the current or the previous one.

## Link to aggregation broadcast

- **The subnet count comes from the other line.** How many subnets a phase has, and how many validators each holds, follows from what one aggregator can prove and how proofs are merged.
- **At a fixed N, a node's vote load does not depend on the subnet count.** A node receives j·n = N·v / P signatures per phase, before gossip duplicates. Cutting the same votes into more subnets changes that only if N changes.
  - Example with the numbers of [`research/pq/`](../research/pq/README.md): P = 8,000 nodes on j = 2 of 64 subnets give N = 250 and 2 × 1,400 signatures per slot. On 16 subnets the same j gives N = 1,000 and four times the load.
    - `external` 8,000 nodes is that model's default. With 13,000, the count behind today's 400 to 500 ([discord F19](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)), the same j gives N = 406 on 64 subnets and 1,625 on 16 (the agent's arithmetic).
- **An aggregator joins every subnet it covers.** One that covers d subnets receives d·n signatures per phase.
  - `external` Reference spec: one subnet carries 16 committees in a slot, about 6,250 votes at 800,000 validators, which is four times what an aggregator of the `pq` spec proves (the agent's arithmetic, from the figures under [Terms](#terms)). An aggregator there can cover a few committees and stay on one topic.
