# Aggregation broadcast

**What it is.** The stage after [attestation broadcast](attestation-broadcast.md). It takes the votes that reached the subnets and delivers them to every node, and to the next proposers, as a few proofs.

**What we want.** Safety: nothing is delivered that was not signed. Liveness: what was signed and sent on time is delivered, as much of it as possible, against each adversary of the model. Then [performance](#performant). Each property is tested against a [baseline](#baseline-todays-p2p-stack): today's p2p stack.

**Origin tags.** Right after its name, every property carries a tag that says where it comes from: `human`, `human-inspired`, `agent-inspired`, `agent` or `external` ([legend](README.md#origin-tags)). A tag covers what the property asks for. The formal definition under it, the figures and "where we are" are an agent's unless they say otherwise.

## Model

`human` (Yann, 2026-11-25) The number of subnets is not fixed. It follows from the aggregation strategy, so everything is stated in parameters.

### Votes and time

- **Slot.** The unit of time. In each slot one cohort of validators votes, and its aggregators then prove and publish. Where several cohorts vote within a slot, as with the three units of [Q17](../research/fg/pipelined-units/README.md), read "phase" for "slot".
- **Round.** One voting pass of the whole validator set, over several slots ([requirements](../design/requirements.md)). It is the **scope** of the finality threshold.
  - `external` Reference spec: a round has 8 slots, and the finality gadget counts per height, not per round. "A quorum of target votes justifies the target and opens the next height" ([`beacon-chain.md` L252–L263](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/beacon-chain.md?plain=1#L252-L263)), and what counts is what blocks have included. A validator signs one target vote per height: its signing record is kept "independently of whether its attestations are included on chain" ([`validator.md` L112–L116](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/validator.md?plain=1#L112-L116)), and it "cannot vote again while a height lasts across rounds" ([`beacon-chain.md` L2804–L2807](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/beacon-chain.md?plain=1#L2804-L2807)). So a vote that no proof picked up is not sent again. It can only be included late.
- **Subnet.** n validators that vote in the same slot and gossip their signatures on one topic. A slot has S subnets. A subnet's weight is the stake of its validators.
  - `external` Reference spec: a slot has 256 attestation committees on 16 subnets. A validator's committee is its index modulo 2,048, and "A validator's committee index is fixed by its validator index, and so is its subnet" ([`validator.md` L280–L283](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/validator.md?plain=1#L280-L283), [`beacon-chain.md` L721–L730](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/beacon-chain.md?plain=1#L721-L730), [`p2p-interface.md` L65–L71](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/p2p-interface.md?plain=1#L65-L71)). At 800,000 validators that is about 390 validators per committee, and 16 committees with about 6,250 votes on each subnet in a slot (the agent's arithmetic). An aggregate there covers committees. So in this model's terms the committee is what an aggregator covers, with S = 256 and n = 390 per slot, and the subnet's topic is what it listens to (the agent's reading). Not upstream: there the committees are an even split of the active validators "in index order rotated by `epoch`" ([upstream `beacon-chain.md` L1962–L1964](https://github.com/ethereum/consensus-specs/blob/bde2349e1e86763aed845e8a064cb22805628e0c/specs/_features/pq/beacon-chain.md?plain=1#L1962-L1964)), so a validator changes committee and subnet about every 390 epochs at 800,000 validators (the agent's arithmetic). The counts and sizes are the same.
  - `external` Committees are not sampled by balance, so subnets differ in weight once balances differ ([ethresear.ch 27](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). Figures here that count subnets as equal are then approximations.
  - `external` How many validators there are is an input that practitioners want capped: "a very generous cap such as 500k or 250k" ([discord F38](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).
- **Nodes on a subnet.** The machines subscribed to the subnet's topic: those that back it and those with a duty on it. Their number is N in [attestation broadcast](attestation-broadcast.md), today about 400 to 500 with default nodes on 2 of the 64 subnets (Yann). It is not n. A validator votes on a subnet without its node being on the topic, and one node can run many validators.
  - `external` The figure has a derivation: with 13,000 nodes on 2 of 64 subnets "we're looking at ~400 beacon nodes as mesh members of every attnet, plus aggregators + observers, maybe 500?" ([discord F19](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). In 2023, with 4,200 nodes measured and before nodes moved to 2 of 64, a subnet also had about 500, and the change was expected to bring it under 200 ([specs 10](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). So it moves with the node count and with the subscription rule.
- **Timely vote.** A vote that reached every honest aggregator of its subnet before that aggregator's cutoff. Getting it there is [attestation broadcast](attestation-broadcast.md)'s job. This stage owes something only to timely votes.
  - `external` Reference spec: a validator makes its FG attestation "at or after the support cutoff", which is half way through the slot, and the deadline after that is a TODO ([`validator.md` L709–L719](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/validator.md?plain=1#L709-L719), [`fork-choice.md` L135–L141](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/fork-choice.md?plain=1#L135-L141)).
  - `external` The cutoff is each aggregator's own: its publishing time minus its proving time, so it depends on the machine ([discord F21](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)), and the `pq` spec staggers four cutoffs per committee ([specs 23](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). A vote can be timely for some of its aggregators and not for others.
  - `external` Practitioners place today's losses at this hand-over: "the main reason for missed head votes today is not being included in aggregates" ([discord F14](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). [Reach](#reach) is the property that asks for more.
- **Delivered.** `human` (the three levels are Yann's, 2026-11-25) A vote is delivered to a node when the node holds a valid proof that covers it. There are three levels, each with its own deadline:
  1. **To the proposer.** The next proposer holds a good aggregate in time to include it: by the end of the aggregation phase, 4 s in [Q17](../research/fg/pipelined-units/README.md), 2 s in [Q19](../research/fg/free-running-committees/README.md). With late inclusion a later proposer can still include it, until the round ends.
     - `external` Valid on gossip is not includable: a proposer checks an aggregate against its own chain again ([specs 36](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)).
     - `external` The `pq` spec gives the proposer a slot more than the nodes: its two-slot inclusion delay "gives the proposer an extra slot to receive aggregates and merge their proofs into the block's proof", while aggregators keep their deadline, "so fork choice sees votes as early as before" ([specs 25](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)).
     - `external` Which deadline pays is open in the specs. The rewards proposed for EIP-8453 pay in full for inclusion in the next slot and nothing after 16 slots ([specs 31](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md), an open pull request). Its branch lets a vote be included from the finalized epoch to the current one ([specs 30](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)).
     - `external` Reference spec: an aggregate can be included "only while its target is the current or the previous target of the inclusion state" ([`validator.md` L1074–L1077](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/validator.md?plain=1#L1074-L1077)). That is not the end of the round. A vote for the previous target counts only towards a pending justification and "credits no flag or reward" ([`beacon-chain.md` L3335–L3346](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/beacon-chain.md?plain=1#L3335-L3346)). Rewards are settled per round, for the round in which the vote is included, and "There are no penalties" ([`beacon-chain.md` L281–L286](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/beacon-chain.md?plain=1#L281-L286)). Nothing in `process_attestation` depends on how late a vote is included (the agent's reading of the function). The decaying rewards of [specs 31](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md) are a pull request against the upstream branch and are not in the reference. Not upstream: there a vote can be included while its data "names the target checkpoint of `state`, or is a vote below the current height whose finality pair is pending" ([upstream `beacon-chain.md` L1608–L1609](https://github.com/ethereum/consensus-specs/blob/bde2349e1e86763aed845e8a064cb22805628e0c/specs/_features/pq/beacon-chain.md?plain=1#L1608-L1609)), and such a late vote still sets the finality flag (the agent's reading of `process_attestation`). There are no penalties there either, and nothing there depends on how late a vote is included.
  2. **To all nodes.** Every node sees the proof by a certain time. That time is more relaxed in DC than in Gasper, because these attestations do not play into fork choice. In Gasper "slot N's votes have to reach every node before block N+1 does" ([builders note](../posts/pq-gasper-builders.md)).
     - `external` It holds in the reference spec, which Yann named on 2026-11-26: "The store follows the finality gadget (FG) through the beacon state and runs the available chain (AC) on head votes" ([`fork-choice.md` L104–L106](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/fork-choice.md?plain=1#L104-L106)). An FG attestation carries "neither a slot nor a block root" ([`p2p-interface.md` L1031–L1040](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/p2p-interface.md?plain=1#L1031-L1040)). The fork-choice handlers take blocks, payloads, payload attestations and head vote messages, and no FG attestation (the agent's reading of the list of handlers). The Simplex pyspec, the reference until then, differs: [specs 33](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md) has a finality attestation update a node's latest-head view when the wire message arrives. Upstream's `pq-dc` has the same sentence and the same handlers ([upstream `fork-choice.md` L92–L94](https://github.com/ethereum/consensus-specs/blob/bde2349e1e86763aed845e8a064cb22805628e0c/specs/_features/pq/fork-choice.md?plain=1#L92-L94)), and the EIP's draft says it outright: "only inclusion in an accepted block changes the finality chain state" ([EIP-8453 draft L35](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/645e812ee79729dc955ea45f1597245d4d72b002/EIPS/eip-8453.md?plain=1#L35)).
     - `agent` So in the reference spec the second level is the block for these votes: a node needs the block's merged proof, and no aggregate before it. What has to reach every node on the wire there is the head vote of the available chain, on its own global topic: what a block says about head votes is "used only if the store holds the signed vote" ([`fork-choice.md` L107–L113](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/fork-choice.md?plain=1#L107-L113)). An aggregate of FG attestations has to reach proposers, which is the first level. Not upstream: there a block carries the head votes themselves, "`process_stark` proves the signatures of the head votes in a block" ([upstream `fork-choice.md` L99–L100](https://github.com/ethereum/consensus-specs/blob/bde2349e1e86763aed845e8a064cb22805628e0c/specs/_features/pq/fork-choice.md?plain=1#L99-L100)), and fork choice reads them from blocks as well (the agent's reading of `get_view_from_blocks`).
     - `external` For a node without a duty this level can be the block itself: "Full nodes can simply not care about P2P attestations and just take them from blocks" ([discord F35](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).
     - `external` Others argue the same: finality "proceeds in parallel with the dynamically available component, off the critical path". One forum reply puts the peak at single-digit Mbit/s per node for 1M validators, proofs of about 350 KiB and a budget of 48 s; it publishes no model ([ethresear.ch 12](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)).
  3. **Available.** The proof sits where a proposer can pick it up if it wants to: in the mempool, or in a blob.
     - `human` (Yann, 2026-11-26) The blob meant here is the blob of a blob transaction: "blobs are a specific type of transaction", and "they are stored for say 2 weeks only".
     - `external` Today a node serves blob data for 4,096 epochs, about 18 days, and a block carries at most 21 blobs (`MIN_EPOCHS_FOR_DATA_COLUMN_SIDECARS_REQUESTS` and `BLOB_SCHEDULE`, consensus-specs @ `d2021d2`, `configs/mainnet.yaml`). Blob data reaches the consensus layer's column subnets with the block that includes it. The draft Yann pointed to changes that last point: blob streaming lets blob data travel ahead of the block, on column subnets of its own, for senders that hold a ticket bought on chain in advance, so that "there is at any time a globally agreed upon bounded set of eligible blob senders" ([EIP-8256 L26](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/bdc1d099649eefb8790b76f912e25487757f5f4e/EIPS/eip-8256.md?plain=1#L26), a draft). The [blob note](../research/pq-aggregation/blob-propagation/README.md#5-blob-transactions-and-blob-streaming) reads it against this file.
     - `external` No source read has consensus aggregates carried in blobs: the Discord archive has no message on it, and the searches on ethresear.ch found no post. One caution from the Discord: blob columns and the last slot's proofs peak on the same uplink, "they will be competing with aggregates from the previous slot still being propagated" ([discord F37](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).
     - `external` The Simplex pyspec asks nodes to keep the signed aggregates they validated and to gossip them again when a proposer needs them, because a block carries an aggregate without "the aggregator wrapper and its signature" ([specs 36](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)).

### Aggregators

- **Aggregator.** A role in one slot, with a coverage set of d subnets. It proves up to L = d·n signatures in one proof.
  - `external` Designs outside this model exist. Nobody is assigned, and every node folds what it holds on each tick ([ethresear.ch 9](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). An operator with many validators proves its own votes before anything is gossiped ([ethresear.ch 22](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). The role is opt-in, opt-out or registered instead of drawn from all validators ([ethresear.ch 1](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md), [discord F31](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). They are listed in the [design space](../research/pq-aggregation/README.md#design-space).
  - `external` EIP-8292, Post-Quantum Attestation Aggregators, read on 2026-11-26, states the opt-in role itself: "A node MAY opt in as an aggregator", and "Aggregation is not a separately selected validator duty, and opting in MUST NOT be gated on a protocol-defined stake threshold" ([EIP-8292 L48](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/68898156500eea7389cf65d746b64c7dd27e4bc1/EIPS/eip-8292.md?plain=1#L48)). A node with enough stake "SHOULD act as a *super-aggregator*" on every subnet ([EIP-8292 L52](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/68898156500eea7389cf65d746b64c7dd27e4bc1/EIPS/eip-8292.md?plain=1#L52)). The EIP is a draft for the lean consensus spec. The reference spec leaves selection open.
- **Replication k.** The number of aggregators whose coverage set contains a given subnet.
- **Instance.** One slot's assignment of A aggregators to S subnets, with A·d = S·k (proved).
- **Machine.** Roles run on machines. One machine can hold several roles, in one slot or across slots, and they are silenced together. The figures below count aggregators. They are machines when every aggregator runs on its own. Validators are not spread evenly over machines (Yann), so with 1,024 aggregators per slot many share one, and with 64 a few do ([baseline](#baseline-todays-p2p-stack)).
- **Merge.** One proof for the union of what up to m child proofs cover. In leanVM today m = 16, a proof can add raw signatures to its children, children may overlap, and a node may publish any subset of what it verified (leanVM @ `20a75c1`: [`10-ethereum.tex`](https://github.com/leanEthereum/leanVM/blob/20a75c102288b6016b5ff8057f8c51c80a222f77/doc/leanvm/body/10-ethereum.tex), [`bindings/rust/README.md`](https://github.com/leanEthereum/leanVM/blob/20a75c102288b6016b5ff8057f8c51c80a222f77/bindings/rust/README.md?plain=1#L65), read 2026-11-25).
  - `external` Client developers were told the same: up to 16 child proofs and raw signatures, and "It outputs the union of the signers; if a signer appears twice, they are included and counted only once" ([discord F22](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). A merge does not check its inputs natively, so a merger verifies each child first, 10 to 21 ms, or risks a wasted proof ([discord F22](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md), [discord F28](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).
  - `external` 16 is not the only limit. Merge time steps up with the committed size: on a machine treated as a low-end aggregator a merge holds 5 children before a step of 1.4 to 1.8 times ([ethresear.ch 3](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)).
- **Redundant assignment.** Aggregators that share one subnet share all of them: the S subnets fall into groups of d, and each group has its own k aggregators, which all prove the same votes. One subnet per aggregator (d = 1) is today's pipeline and the `pq` spec ([design note](../research/pq-gasper/design.md)).
- **Dispersed assignment.** No two aggregators of a subnet have the same coverage set, which needs d ≥ 2. This is Yann's "erasure-coded" aggregation: an aggregator takes d whole subnets, at random or round robin, so that about S/d proofs cover the slot, and d·n is no more than an aggregator proves in the `pq` spec.
- **Layer.** One pass of a round robin: S/d aggregators that take d subnets each and together take every subnet once. k layers give replication k, and the proofs of one layer are the children of a full merge.
- **Overlap λ.** The most aggregators that two subnets share. It measures how dispersed an assignment is, and it is what the bounds depend on. A redundant assignment with d ≥ 2 has λ = k. The affine-plane example of the [workstream](../research/pq-aggregation/README.md) has λ = 1. So have layers that follow the directions of a grid, and sliding windows have λ = d − 1.

**What an honest aggregator does.**

1. It collects the votes of its coverage set until the cutoff.
2. It proves every valid vote it collected.
3. It publishes the proof on time, to everyone.

`external` A proof can cover votes that differ, where a BLS aggregator takes only votes equal to its own ([ethresear.ch 17](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). So step 2 is a real duty under PQ, and what a proof shows is a set of signers for each vote value.

### Types of aggregator

`human` Yann named the types on 2026-11-25: "aggregators that want to maliciously reduce yield of some validators, and thus don't include their attestations", a rational aggregator, "probably in between", and "the Byzantine one of course, doing the worst thing possible". The table's wording and the points under it are the agent's. Each type is defined by which of the three steps it may break, and why.

| Type | What it does | How many | Property against it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Honest | The three steps, whatever they cost it | | [Complete](#complete) |
| Rational | Whatever pays it most. It skips the proof, leaves votes out, times its proof or shows it to one proposer only, when that pays. It deviates for no other reason | Possibly all | [Incentive compatibility](#incentive-compatibility) |
| Censoring | The three steps, except that it leaves out the votes of the validators it targets, to cut their yield. Its proof looks like an honest one | A share β of stake | [Censorship resistance](#censorship-resistance) |
| Byzantine | Anything, together with the other Byzantine parties and the network adversary: publish nothing, leave votes out, publish late or to some nodes only, publish many proofs. It cannot forge a signature or a proof | A share β of stake | [Safety](#safety) always; [Byzantine tolerance](#byzantine-tolerance) |

- **Honest, censoring and Byzantine form a line.** Each may do what the one before it does, and more.
- **Rational is not a point on that line.** It is a motive, not a set of permitted moves. Every rational deviation is one a Byzantine aggregator could make too, so in what it does it sits between honest and Byzantine. But all aggregators may be rational at once, so the k aggregators of a subnet are no protection against it. Rewards have to be.
- **From stake to roles.** Under a stake-weighted draw each role is censoring, or Byzantine, with probability β. That takes roles as drawn independently; a draw without replacement gives slightly less.
- **Silent.** An aggregator is silent in a slot when it publishes nothing: it crashed, was silenced, or chose to. For liveness that is the worst a single aggregator can do, as long as safety and no displacement hold (below).
- **Mergers and proposers.** A merger is an aggregator of proofs, and the same types apply to it. The proposer is the last party on a vote's path. With several levels, a vote is sure to be delivered when one path from its subnet to the proposer has no faulty party on it.
- **What the sources add.** `external`
  - **An aggregator can equivocate** without any vote equivocating: "An aggregator can produce two valid aggregates for the same slot, differing in the attesting indices". Today only the first one from an aggregator is forwarded, which bounds the load and splits views ([ethresear.ch 24](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)).
  - **Announce and withhold.** Where others hold back on the strength of an announced proof, an aggregator that announces and never serves does more harm than a silent one ([ethresear.ch 6](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md); the post raises it for raw transactions in a mempool and has not tested a rule against it, and the step to aggregators is an agent's).
  - **A rational attester** chooses when to send: "rational attesters may delay their attestation deadline to vote accurately" ([ethresear.ch 29](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md), [specs 14](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). The types above describe aggregators only.
  - **Silencing can pay.** A validator gains when the duty holder before it fails, and known duty holders can be bribed ([ethresear.ch 34](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). The network adversary below holds no stake.
  - **The role may not be drawn by stake.** With an opt-in, opt-out or registered role, "From stake to roles" no longer holds as written. The requirement stated for that case is a set "decentralized enough to guarantee one honest validator per committee in each slot" ([discord F31](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).

### Network adversary

`human` Yann, 2026-11-25: "the network adversary, that can crash nodes (or simply disconnect them from the network)", "measured by machines attacked at any given time (which I think can be simplified to machines attacked per slot)". What it knows, what the simplification assumes and what it cannot do are the agent's. It is not an aggregator: it holds no stake and no keys. If it also holds stake, it is this adversary together with Byzantine aggregators.

- **What it knows.** Which machine holds which role in each slot. That is the worst case: the draw is public or can be watched, and the adversary has a map from validators to machines ([aggregator selection](../research/pq-gasper/aggregator-selection.md)).
- **What it does.** It silences machines: it crashes them, or cuts them off from the network. A silenced machine sends and receives nothing.
- **Its budget c.** At most c machines silenced at any given time. Simplified to **c machines per slot**: in every slot it picks at most c machines after seeing that slot's assignment, silences them for the slot, and picks again in the next.
- **What the simplification assumes.** That silencing a machine for one slot is what it takes to lose that slot's roles on it. No less: the attack cannot move from one aggregator to the next inside a slot. No more: the role cannot be made up after the slot. Late inclusion breaks the second part in our favour.
- **What it cannot do.** Slow down or reorder messages between machines it has not silenced. A slow or lossy network is "Reliable" in the [list of properties](README.md).
- **What the sources add.** `external`
  - **An adversary that joins.** With many nodes, no stake and nobody to silence it can crowd one subnet, because node IDs decide who backs which subnet: "An attack on a single subnet would require just 1/64 of malicious nodes than attacking the whole network" ([specs 11](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). It belongs to [attestation broadcast](attestation-broadcast.md).
  - **Relays that do not forward.** Nodes that sit on a topic and drop what they receive were suspected of costing votes on a testnet in 2020 ([discord F11](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).
  - **Failures that correlate.** "If all the aggregators in the committee are from these clients, the attestation is likely to be missing" ([specs 14](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). A fault of one client's software is neither random nor chosen by an adversary.
  - **A slot of silence can cost more than a slot.** If peers cache the top layer of a signature to halve vote traffic, "a freshly started node needs ~10 epochs to fill its cache, so until then it can't do a complete job as an aggregator" ([discord F39](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). That goes against what the simplification assumes.
  - **What it knows** is supported: "by default it is possible to link IPs to validator public keys if you listen to gossip long enough" ([discord F8](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).

## Baseline: today's p2p stack

`human` The properties are tested against the current p2p stack (Yann, 2026-11-25): "There are 16 randomly chosen aggregators in a subnet of about 400 nodes, and 64 subnets." The entries of the table are the agent's reading and modelling.

- **What it is.** 64 attestation subnets, with one committee on each in every slot. A private lottery targets 16 aggregators per committee, and each aggregates its own committee. An aggregate is a BLS signature and a bitlist, sent on one global topic that every node joins. (consensus-specs @ `d2021d2`, read 2026-11-25: [`phase0/p2p-interface.md`](https://github.com/ethereum/consensus-specs/blob/d2021d2719235f0df4726a1d330502cb15aa8ea3/specs/phase0/p2p-interface.md?plain=1#L307) lines 307 and 616, [`phase0/validator.md`](https://github.com/ethereum/consensus-specs/blob/d2021d2719235f0df4726a1d330502cb15aa8ea3/specs/phase0/validator.md?plain=1#L733) lines 105 and 733.)

## Safety

`human` Nothing false is delivered, and no honest node is overloaded, whatever aggregators and the network adversary do. Safety is not a matter of chance, beyond the proof system's own soundness. That safety and liveness are the two overarching properties is Yann's (2026-11-25: "the overarching properties should be safety and liveness of aggregation dissemination"); what each holds is tagged below.

- **Integrity.** `human-inspired` (safety is Yann's; that it means integrity here is the agent's) If an honest node delivers a vote, its validator signed it. A vote counts once, however many proofs cover it.
  - It holds against any number of Byzantine aggregators, because a node delivers only what it has verified. It rests on the signature scheme and the proof system, not on the strategy.
  - So this stage can leave votes out but cannot make any up. The finality gadget's safety needs nothing more from it; the gadget's liveness needs everything below.
  - `external` It has a precondition in what the proof states. An open issue on the `pq` branch says that leanVM "currently produces proofs that attest only `(pubkey, message)` pairs", without the leaf index of the signature, so that one reused leaf could let an aggregator or a proposer forge a vote ([specs 27](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). Its author has not assessed how practical that is.
  - `external` The formal version is the unforgeability of a signature-merge scheme: nobody can produce a valid merged proof whose signer set holds an honest validator that did not sign ([ethresear.ch 15](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md), [ethresear.ch 22](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)).
- **Accountable.** `external` Who signed what stays provable when only proofs are left. "Because the consensus layer must know exactly who participated to process rewards, inactivity leaks, fork choice weight, and slashing, the aggregate payload includes both the leanVM proof and a signer bitfield", and evidence against a validator has to be kept for as long as slashing can use it ([ethresear.ch 15](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). One bit per validator is therefore the cost that aggregation cannot remove ([ethresear.ch 14](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). Kept by Yann on 2026-11-26.
- **Equivocation evidence travels.** `external` Gossip forwards a second, different vote from the same validator, because "dropping the second vote on gossip prevented nodes from detecting head-vote equivocations over P2P" ([specs 32](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md): an open pull request on the EIP-8453 branch, for head votes; the Simplex pyspec does the same for finality votes, per round). The bound per validator and round is then two messages, not one. An aggregator that publishes two valid proofs for one slot is the same case one level up, and today its second proof is dropped ([ethresear.ch 24](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). Kept by Yann on 2026-11-26. Reference spec: it holds for head votes, "Forward at most two distinct valid vote data values per validator and slot, so one equivocation can propagate" ([`p2p-interface.md` L550–L552](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/p2p-interface.md?plain=1#L550-L552)). For FG attestations gossip forwards one per validator and round for the current target ([`p2p-interface.md` L1106–L1110](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/p2p-interface.md?plain=1#L1106-L1110)), so a second, different one does not travel there. Not upstream: there gossip forwards only "the first valid head vote from this validator for the slot" ([upstream `p2p-interface.md` L537](https://github.com/ethereum/consensus-specs/blob/bde2349e1e86763aed845e8a064cb22805628e0c/specs/_features/pq/p2p-interface.md?plain=1#L537)), and the second is the pull request above.
- **Sybil resilience.** `agent-inspired` (an agent proposed "bounded load"; Yann took it up on 2026-11-25 as "Sybil resilience / DoS resilience of the different topics") On every topic, what an honest node forwards and verifies per slot stays under a bound that nobody can raise by creating identities, with cheap checks before the proof check ([Q15](../research/open_questions.md)).
  - So the senders of a topic have to be a small set tied to stake or to the assignment. A set that anyone can join does not work (Yann). The [builders note](../posts/pq-gasper-builders.md) makes the point for builders: "The set of staked builders is open-ended, so the topic could be spammed to keep votes from arriving in time".
  - This is the denial of service that comes through the topics. The one on machines is [DoS resilience](#dos-resilience).
  - In the `pq` spec the bound is the sender set: non-members are rejected, and each aggregator gets one aggregate per epoch ([design note](../research/pq-gasper/design.md)).
  - `external` The bound has two parts: who may send, and how many different valid messages one sender can make. A proof over any subset of the votes is another valid message, and "an aggregator could add attestations to the aggregate one at a time and so wind up making the aggregate topic have the same traffic as the single attestation topic" ([discord F17](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md), [discord F5](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). So a small sender set needs a budget of messages per sender next to it.
  - `external` The order of checks: tie the message to a staked signer first, then do the work that depends on the state, then check the proof ([specs 20](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). Whoever causes the work is then known.
  - `external` What identifies a message has to be signed, or follow from what is. In the EIP-8453 branch: "Gossip deduplication must use the data and signers, since the epoch is not signed" ([specs 29](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). leanVM proofs "have a single encoding, so they can be identified by their bytes" ([discord F22](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). The reference spec asks the same of aggregates, in a TODO: "Gossip deduplication must use the data, round and signers" ([`validator.md` L1071–L1072](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/validator.md?plain=1#L1071-L1072)). Upstream's `pq-dc` has the TODO without the round: "Gossip deduplication must use the data and signers" ([upstream `validator.md` L901](https://github.com/ethereum/consensus-specs/blob/bde2349e1e86763aed845e8a064cb22805628e0c/specs/_features/pq/validator.md?plain=1#L901)).
  - `external` A counter-example to "a set that anyone can join does not work". In the Simplex pyspec any node may aggregate on the topic of the available committee, where "Gossip superset rules suppress redundant aggregates" ([specs 35](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). On its finality aggregates gossip ignores "every aggregate that adds none", meaning no new signer evidence, "even when it uses a previously unseen aggregator" ([specs 34](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). Both bound content in place of senders. That carries over to proofs only if what a proof covers can be read before the proof is fetched and checked ([ethresear.ch 5](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)).
  - `external` A third way to bound the senders of a topic, next to stake and assignment: payment in advance. In the blob streaming draft "holding a ticket is a prerequisit for the network to propagate one's blob data" ([EIP-8256 L26](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/bdc1d099649eefb8790b76f912e25487757f5f4e/EIPS/eip-8256.md?plain=1#L26)). A ticket is bought on chain, and "Ticket fees are burned at purchase time with no refund mechanism" ([EIP-8256 L440](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/bdc1d099649eefb8790b76f912e25487757f5f4e/EIPS/eip-8256.md?plain=1#L440)). Gossip takes "Only the first valid sidecar per `(ticket_id, column_index)`" ([EIP-8256 L396](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/bdc1d099649eefb8790b76f912e25487757f5f4e/EIPS/eip-8256.md?plain=1#L396)). The sender set stays open and bounded. Whether that fits an aggregator is in the [blob note](../research/pq-aggregation/blob-propagation/README.md#5-blob-transactions-and-blob-streaming).
  - `external` EIP-8292 keeps the aggregator set open on purpose: "This keeps the role permissionless while making the hardware requirement explicit: the protocol relies on enough capable nodes choosing to aggregate" ([EIP-8292 L127](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/68898156500eea7389cf65d746b64c7dd27e4bc1/EIPS/eip-8292.md?plain=1#L127)). It is the opposite choice to the one at the top of this list, and what bounds the messages of such a set is left to the lean consensus spec.
  - `external` What was tried and dropped. A cap per sender on unsigned aggregates let a neighbour "block the original good aggregate from making it far in the gossip" with a worse one, and "forward only if better" let one sender climb from small to large. Hence signed aggregates, one per aggregator ([specs 4](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)).
  - `external` The caches behind these rules have to live as long as a vote stays includable. When the inclusion window grew to two epochs, replayed aggregates led to "a 5x increase in aggregate traffic" ([specs 18](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)).
  - `external` Valid votes can overload too. In May 2023 attestations with old targets made two clients rebuild old states again and again, and finality was delayed twice ([ethresear.ch 19](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). The costly check there was not a signature, and what to drop under overload is "a question about the whole system".

## Liveness

Every timely vote is delivered, at each level by that level's deadline.

- **A matter of degree.** `human-inspired` (Yann: "we want as many attestations being part of the aggregate as possible") No design keeps that promise for every vote once aggregators fail. **Coverage** is the share, by stake, of a slot's or a round's timely votes that are delivered by the deadline, and **loss** is the rest. We want coverage as high as it can be: every vote left out costs its validator yield, finality needs two thirds of all stake, and a safety gadget needs close to all of it ([properties](README.md), "Enables a safety gadget").
- **A matter of chance.** `human-inspired` (Yann: "I reckon its probabilistic") Who aggregates is drawn at random, and some failures strike at random. So a liveness guarantee is one of two probabilities:
  - **Per vote:** the probability that a given timely vote is not delivered. A validator's yield depends on it.
  - **Per round:** the probability that a round's loss exceeds q, the share finality can spare. q = 1/3 when every validator votes. With a share β of stake withholding its votes, q = 1 − 2 / (3(1 − β)).
- **Against an adversary that picks its targets, the guarantee is a worst case.** `agent` It is a budget below which the adversary cannot succeed, whatever it picks. The network adversary is of this kind when it knows the draw.
- **One honest aggregator is enough.** `agent` Without a fallback, a timely vote is sure to be delivered if, and only if, at least one aggregator of its subnet is honest and not silenced. Each property below says, for its adversary, what it takes to spoil all k. The rule rests on a property of its own:
  - `external` As a rule it is the stated reason for today's 16: "We currently elect 16 aggregators to be almost certain that we will never end up without at least one honest aggregator" ([ethresear.ch 21](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). As a target a margin is asked for; it should not be "as long as 1 out of 16 can do a good enough job, we're happy" ([discord F18](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).
  - **No displacement.** `agent` A vote that an honest aggregator proved and published on time is delivered. No rule on the way drops it in favour of a proof that lacks it: not gossip deduplication, not a later aggregator that stays silent, not a merge that takes one child of several.
    - `external` It has been broken and defended before. The superset rule ignores only what one seen aggregate covers ([specs 6](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). The `pq` branch forwarded only the heavier aggregate for a day and went back, "so every aggregate that adds a vote is still propagated" ([specs 26](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). Dropping small aggregates for a bigger one not yet seen was called "a very gray liveness problem area" in 2019 ([discord F4](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).
    - `external` It fails wherever one aggregate per subnet is picked by weight. A malicious aggregator "can always create the best aggregate by stealing the second best aggregate", dropping honest signers and adding its own ([ethresear.ch 20](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). A relay that forwards only its best proof "can strand a proof that covers transactions missing from the best proof" ([ethresear.ch 7](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). Staggered aggregators that stay silent when they "differ by a few signatures" apply the same rule with a threshold ([discord F27](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).
- **Loss unit.** `agent` The smallest share of a slot's votes that can go missing together. Smaller is better: rewards and participation degrade in small steps.
  - `external` The argument has been made at 1 of 64: losing every aggregator of one committee was called negligible "Given that there are 64 committees" ([ethresear.ch 26](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). It is four times weaker at the `pq` spec's 16.

### Complete

`human` (the requirement is Yann's; the name is the agent's) With honest aggregators and nothing silenced, coverage is 1: "Aggregation is required — votes must land on chain" ([requirements](../design/requirements.md)).

- `external` Measured without an adversary: the first PQ devnet had "86% attesting on gossip, 77% included on chain" in its twelfth epoch ([discord F34](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). With BLS, at genesis, "mainnet attestation aggregation is being _ludicrously_ effective" ([discord F10](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).

### Reach

`external` Every vote sent on time reaches an aggregate, with everyone honest. "What you want is not too guarantee that there's one aggregate, you want a guarantee that every single attestation will reach an aggregate" ([discord F14](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). Kept by Yann on 2026-11-26.

- **It is the part that "timely vote" leaves to attestation broadcast,** and it is where votes are lost today.
- **The number of aggregators counts as places to arrive at,** not only as spares against faults: "At 4 per subnet it'll be much harder to find them".
- **Under a lottery it includes the draw.** A target of 4 leaves a subnet with no aggregator 1.8 % of the time, about 36 committees per epoch, and with exactly one 7 % of the time ([discord F23](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). A draw of exactly k removes that; the `pq` spec at `4c29678` has one.

### DoS resilience

`human` Against the network adversary. Yann named the property and its measure on 2026-11-25: "I reckon we want fault tolerance. The specific property should be called DoS resilience". The outcomes and t(q) below are the agent's formalisation.

- **Definition.** The DoS resilience against an outcome is the fewest machines per slot that the network adversary must silence to bring the outcome about. Any smaller budget fails. Higher is better.
- **Outcome: one vote.** Keep a chosen validator's vote out for one slot. It takes the machines that hold the k aggregators of its subnet: k machines under any assignment, or fewer if one machine holds several of them (proved).
- **Outcome: finality.** Leave more than a share q of a round's timely votes undelivered.
  - The budget holds in each slot on its own, and the losses of the slots add up. So a round of alike slots breaks at the same budget as one slot. (The agent's derivation, checked by script on small cases: [dispersed note](../research/pq-aggregation/dispersed-assignment/README.md), "Failures counted at one time".)
  - That budget is **t(q)**: the fewest aggregators of one instance whose silence leaves more than a share q of the slot's votes without a proof. t(q) depends only on which aggregator covers which subnet (proved).
  - When slots differ, it is the least c for which the largest losses that c silenced machines can cause in each slot add up to more than a share q of the round.
  - `external` Reference spec: the quorum is counted per height, over the votes that blocks have included, and no vote is sent twice ([Round](#votes-and-time)). The budget argument above stands as long as a lost vote stays lost. Late inclusion is what breaks it in our favour (the agent's reading).
- **Earlier names**, still used in the workstream notes and in Lean: per-vote tolerance is the first outcome, threshold tolerance is t(q), and the coverage profile is the largest loss for each budget.
- **Two regimes, by what the adversary knows.** `human` Both stay in the design space: each has clear advantages and disadvantages (Yann, 2026-11-25).
  - **Aggregators secret.** The adversary does not know who aggregates. It sees the nodes on a subnet's topic, and the aggregators are among them: 16 out of about 400 (Yann). So it silences nodes blind, and the resilience is the number of machines for a given chance of success, which is close to all the nodes on the subnet. An adversary with a map from validators to machines can silence the machines of the committee's members instead ([aggregator selection](../research/pq-gasper/aggregator-selection.md), "Blind attacker"). Today's lottery is this regime as long as nothing leaks: an aggregator has to join its subnet and other attesters need not, so one that was not on the subnet can be seen joining it (same note, "Watch"). The lottery does not port to leanXMSS as it stands, because a private PQ lottery needs a PQ VRF (same note), and the `pq` spec draws its aggregators publicly. An assignment that has aggregators listen to more subnets has them join more, and so shows more of them (the agent's inference).
  - **Aggregators known.** The guarantee is a worst case: all k aggregators of a subnet for one vote, and t(q) aggregators per slot for finality. Knowing the aggregators in advance reduces security: they can be targeted, and aggregators may be less protected than proposers (Yann). It is the regime in which the assignment matters.
  - `external` **Where today's stack sits.** The spec gives spam, not protection, as the purpose of the private selection ([specs 3](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). The protection was understood in 2020: nobody knows the aggregators "until the aggregator broadcasts their aggregate", against "known 2 epochs in advance" for a public draw ([discord F1](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). In practice the duty shows in the subnet subscription, which an aggregator takes 5 to 20 slots early or for the epoch ([specs 12](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md), [ethresear.ch 31](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)), and secrecy wears off over repeated duties ([ethresear.ch 35](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). One exchange of 2020 does treat the private selection as the answer: to "Seems like aggregators could be DOSed similarly to proposers" the reply was "I think aggregators are already selected privately at random based on a signature" ([ethresear.ch topic 7547, replies 11 and 12](https://ethresear.ch/t/7547/11), read in the downloaded posts by the oversight thread).
  - `external` **A private lottery under PQ has a building block in the `pq` spec.** The secret regime above has a private PQ lottery wait for a PQ VRF. At `4c29678` every validator registers the root of a hash tree with one secret per slot, and a proposer opens its leaf as the RANDAO reveal ([`pq/beacon-chain.md`](https://github.com/ethereum/consensus-specs/blob/4c296784bb6ff54cbdcf72ebdda2a1057cce8893/specs/_features/pq/beacon-chain.md?plain=1#L187-L196), lines 187 to 196, read 2026-11-25). The spec does not use it for aggregators: since 17 November it selects them deterministically, from aggregation committees kept in the state ([`e26cc6f`](https://github.com/ethereum/consensus-specs/commit/e26cc6fe90416184d3f4369cbc49e13ebe9ad411), [specs 28](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)), and the same lines say that aggregators no longer select themselves. A developer raised the tree for them two days later: "For aggregators, we can have multiple vrf openings from different validators" ([discord F33](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). An opening is the slot's secret and 31 hashes, 1,024 bytes by the spec's `VRFProof`, against a proof of 116 KB for the PQ VRF proposed for RANDAO ([ethresear.ch 33](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)); the tree costs about 4.3 billion hashes per validator to build ([discord F33](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). Whether such a lottery is as hard to bias as today's is not discussed in the archive. Reference spec: such openings are in use there, for the head vote committee of the available chain. A validator "casts one head vote if it wins any tickets" ([`validator.md` L290–L294](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/validator.md?plain=1#L290-L294)), its message carries the opening for the slot ([`validator.md` L681–L683](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/validator.md?plain=1#L681-L683)), and "The committee size is an expectation, not a bound" ([`beacon-chain.md` L742](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/beacon-chain.md?plain=1#L742)). So a lottery on the hash tree, secret until the validator votes, is specified for voters. For aggregators nothing is. Not upstream: there the head vote committee has 1,024 seats "sampled by effective balance" ([upstream `beacon-chain.md` L239–L240](https://github.com/ethereum/consensus-specs/blob/bde2349e1e86763aed845e8a064cb22805628e0c/specs/_features/pq/beacon-chain.md?plain=1#L239-L240)) and kept in the state, so it is known before the slot. The EIP's draft leaves it open: "Private selection using a verifiable random function (VRF) is one option being explored; neither the selection mechanism nor the committee size is fixed here" ([EIP-8453 draft L71](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/645e812ee79729dc955ea45f1597245d4d72b002/EIPS/eip-8453.md?plain=1#L71)).
  - `external` **A third regime: mixed.** "2 aggregators selected deterministically, and then some more from VRF" ([discord F23](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). The precedent is a public proposer with secret backups, which names its own problems: backups that publish eagerly, and a backup that gains from an attack on the primary ([ethresear.ch 33](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)).
  - `external` **Structure costs privacy.** In 2019 "any kind of pre-determined network structure" was taken to "throw out the idea of validator privacy", and that is the stated reason today's aggregation has none ([discord F3](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). Layers and grids are such a structure.
  - `external` **The lean devnets are in the known regime.** There the aggregator "is a statically configured opt-in role rather than a secretly drawn duty" ([EIP-8292 L133](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/68898156500eea7389cf65d746b64c7dd27e4bc1/EIPS/eip-8292.md?plain=1#L133)), and against stalling or censoring aggregators "Running redundant aggregators is the primary mitigation" ([EIP-8292 L162](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/68898156500eea7389cf65d746b64c7dd27e4bc1/EIPS/eip-8292.md?plain=1#L162)).
  - `external` **The blind budget is not out of reach.** With secret duty holders "an attacker can indeed enumerate and blind-DDoS the entire set of home stakers", and "this could even potentially be doable" ([ethresear.ch 32](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). The same post names a mitigation: a separate address for the duty.
- **Failures at random are the mild case.** `agent` When each aggregator fails with probability p, independently, a vote is lost with probability p^k under any assignment, and the expected loss is the same for every assignment with replication k (both proved). An assignment changes only which losses come together.
  - `external` Real failures are not independent. They correlate by client software ([specs 14](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)), and in the worst case on record most validators were offline at once. What failed first then was finding aggregators: "I think the biggest one is the inability to find aggregators" ([discord F20](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). The requirement behind it is that the votes of those still online keep being included: with 20 to 30 % online "the active validators were bleeding out only marginally slower then the offline validators" ([discord F20](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). With 10 % online and exactly 4 aggregators per committee, two committees in three have none online (the research note's arithmetic).
- **Target.** `human` (Yann's requirement) "Networking must not be the binding fault bound" ([requirements](../design/requirements.md#load)).
  - **The ideal:** `agent-inspired` (an agent proposed it; Yann: "perhaps that's the ideal target we might not reach") stopping finality through the aggregators takes no fewer machines per slot than stopping it by silencing the voters themselves. It may be out of reach (Yann).
  - **The voters' level, in machines,** is the fewest machines that hold a third of the stake. Validators are not spread evenly over machines (Yann). Under a measured node list it is 22 to 102 machines; the approximations are less concentrated, at 112 to 286 in the shapes after Heimbach et al. and 538 or 1,347 in the shapes this repo's benchmarks run with ([baseline](#baseline-todays-p2p-stack), sources). It is not the 8,334 validators that are a third of a slot's voters, which an earlier version of this file compared with.
  - **The test:** `human` the [baseline](#baseline-todays-p2p-stack) in the same regime.
    - With secret aggregators today's stack costs every node on 22 subnets, 4,600 to 5,750 machines, or 1,236 to 2,713 for an adversary with a map. Both are above the voters' level under every picture, by 26 times or more measured and 2.7 times or more in the approximations. So the aggregators are not the weak point, as long as nothing leaks.
    - With known aggregators it costs 352 aggregators per slot, or 267 with the lottery's counts. Measured, the 352 sit on at most 153 to 238 machines, 2.3 to 7 times the voters' level, so there too the aggregators are not the weak point. In the approximations they sit on 201 to 314: 0.95 to 1.8 times the voters' level after Heimbach et al., and a half or a quarter of it in the two benchmark shapes. The more concentrated the hosting, the lower the voters' level, and the better known aggregators compare with it.
  - **A cap for known aggregators:** `agent` A aggregators never cost more than A machines, and no assignment costs more than about q^(1/k) · A ([dispersed note](../research/pq-aggregation/dispersed-assignment/README.md)). Measured, the `pq` spec's layout breaks at 18 to 21 machines per slot, below the voters' 22 to 102 in every reading. The grid layers break at 29 to 34, which passes the voters' level in the most concentrated reading only.
  - **Machines are not alike.** `agent` The machines that hold a third of the stake are the largest ones. Flooding is "easy against home links and harder against data-center machines behind provider DDoS protection" ([aggregator selection](../research/pq-gasper/aggregator-selection.md)). The count treats all machines alike.
  - `external` **Bandwidth pulls the other way.** In PQ Gasper every node needs the proofs inside the fork-choice window, and "propagating 64 aggregates (~19 Mib without gossipsub amplification) within 5s interval" led to proposals of one aggregator per subnet, answered with "isn't 1 aggregator per subnet super risky?" ([discord F24](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). Where delivery to all nodes can wait, replication does not have to pay that price.
- **Where we are, aggregators known.**
  - `external` **The reference spec** leaves it open: "*TODO*: Specify aggregator selection for head votes and FG attestations" ([`validator.md` L933–L936](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/validator.md?plain=1#L933-L936)). The figures below are for the `pq` spec, which is PQ Gasper, and for designs on the baseline's 64 subnets. Nothing is computed yet for the reference spec's 256 committees on 16 subnets.
  - **Any assignment of one slot:** at least k · (⌊q·S⌋ + 1) / d, about q · A, and the redundant assignment sits there (proved). At most about q^(1/k) · A.
  - **The `pq` spec** draws 4 public aggregators per committee, each on its own committee: 16 committees and 64 aggregators per slot ([`specs/`](../specs/README.md#pq-gasper)). One vote: 4 machines. Finality: 24 machines per slot, which silence 6 of the 16 committees. [Aggregator selection](../research/pq-gasper/aggregator-selection.md) gives the average over an epoch, about 21.
  - **Aggregators on 4 whole subnets** (Yann's design, on the baseline's 64 subnets). An aggregator proves a 16th of the slot's voters, which is exactly a `pq` committee. With the `pq` spec's 64 aggregators, in 4 layers of 16, finality breaks at:

    | How the layers deal the subnets | Machines per slot |
    |---|---:|
    | The same way in every layer, which is the `pq` spec's layout | 24 |
    | Shifted by one subnet per layer (sliding windows) | 25 |
    | No layers: every aggregator draws its 4 subnets on its own | at most 26 to 27 |
    | A fresh shuffle per layer | 30 to 35 |
    | Along another direction of a 4 × 4 × 4 grid per layer | 37 |
    | Ceiling for any assignment of 64 aggregators on 4 subnets | 49 |

    - **The gain depends on the deal.** Round robin has to regroup the subnets in every layer, and random has to be a shuffle within layers. With independent draws some subnet has no aggregator at all in two draws of three.
    - **More resilience with the same aggregators.** The grid layers put two subnets under a common aggregator at most once and break at 37. The `pq` layout reaches that with 7 aggregators per committee, 112 in all.
    - **The same resilience with fewer.** Three grid layers, 48 aggregators, break at 25, against the `pq` spec's 24 with 64. One vote then costs 3 machines instead of 4.
    - **Smaller steps.** 16 silenced machines leave at most 8 of the 64 subnets without a proof, against 16 under the `pq` layout, and 24 leave 12 against 24.
    - **Counted in machines the gain stays.** A machine that hosts several of a slot's aggregators loses them together. Under a measured node list the 64 roles sit on 52 to 60 machines, and finality breaks at 18 to 21 machines under the `pq` layout and at 29 to 34 under the grid layers. In the approximations it is 20 to 24 and 33 to 37. The first of each pair is exact; the second is an attack found, so the true figure is that or lower ([dispersed note](../research/pq-aggregation/dispersed-assignment/README.md#aggregators-on-a-few-whole-subnets)).
    - `external` **What the specs do to it as written.** The `pq` spec at `4c29678` does not carry such an aggregate. Gossip rejects one unless "Exactly one committee is specified by the committee bits" ([specs 19](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)), and a proposer includes one aggregate per committee, without combining two that overlap ([specs 24](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). It also takes one aggregate per aggregator and epoch, which rules out a validator that aggregates and merges, or aggregates twice, within an epoch ([specs 5](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). The EIP-8453 branch states the other direction: "Aggregates combine the attestations of several committees" ([specs 30](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). So does the reference spec: an aggregate marks its committees in `committee_bits`, up to 131,072 validators in all, and its proof has one statement per committee "so that the proposer can merge the proofs of aggregates that cover different committees into the block's `proof`" ([`beacon-chain.md` L2127–L2129](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/beacon-chain.md?plain=1#L2127-L2129)). A block takes up to 16 such aggregates ([`beacon-chain.md` L804–L809](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/beacon-chain.md?plain=1#L804-L809)).
  - **Against today's 352.** Shuffled layers reach it with 512 aggregators, half of today's 1,024, and break at 746 to 773 with all 1,024. But each of them proves four subnets. Per vote proved, today's layout stays ahead: 256 aggregators on 4 subnets prove every vote 16 times, as today's 1,024 do, and break at 162 to 174. Many small aggregators are more machines to silence. Under PQ they are also four times as many proofs to carry and merge.
  - **What these figures are.** [Dispersed note](../research/pq-aggregation/dispersed-assignment/README.md#aggregators-on-a-few-whole-subnets); not in Lean. 24 and the sliding 25 follow from the layout. 37 and the three layers' 25 are exact, from a search that is checked against enumeration. For shuffled layers the lower figure holds for all but one shuffle in 10⁹, and the upper one is an attack found on a draw. All take exactly k aggregators per subnet, a draw and not a lottery.
  - **With secret aggregators** it gains nothing: a blind adversary has to silence every node on a subnet whatever the assignment (the agent's reasoning, not computed). An aggregator on 4 subnets also joins about four times as many topics per duty, which gives a watcher more to see.

### Censorship resistance

`human` Against censoring aggregators. Yann, 2026-11-25: "We also want to model censorship resistance". The definition below is the agent's formalisation.

- **Definition.** For any validator the adversary targets: the probability that its timely vote is not delivered by the deadline, when a share β of stake censors. Lower is better. The worst-case form is the number of aggregators that have to collude.
- **Without a fallback** the vote is kept out only if all k aggregators of its subnet censor. That is k colluding aggregators under any assignment (proved), and β^k per slot under a stake-weighted draw: 1.2 % at β = 1/3 and k = 4.
- **Nothing here relies on catching the censor.** Its proof is valid, and a vote it left out looks the same as a vote that reached it late.
- **The deadline matters.** With late inclusion a vote left out in its slot can be picked up until the round ends, so the censor needs every later aggregator and proposer as well. The [incentives proposal](../design/incentives.md) pulls the same way: the votes nobody has included yet become the most valuable ones.
- **It needs no displacement.** One honest aggregator protects a vote only if its proof is not dropped for the censor's.
- **Where we are.** The `pq` spec at `4c29678` has 4 aggregators per subnet and no fallback. Its gossip keeps the superset rule, which "never drops a vote", and a later aggregator broadcasts unless a received aggregate covers its attesters ([design note](../research/pq-gasper/design.md)). Whether a merge keeps no displacement is open: a merger that takes one proof per subnet has to take one that lacks no vote, or add the missing votes itself, which takes room in the merge.
- **Why it matters beyond yield.** `external` "I'm very skeptical of a system in which the whole finality gadget is gatekeeped by a smaller set of aggregators": votes that nodes saw and the chain did not make the two views differ ([discord F36](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).
- **One censor is enough where a merger picks.** `external` If a merger or a proposer takes one aggregate per subnet by weight, a single aggregator displaces the honest ones, and the proposer "can always choose" ([ethresear.ch 20](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). "All k have to censor" holds only with no displacement all the way into the block.
- **A censor cannot be told from a slow network.** `external` That has been said since 2018 ([ethresear.ch 36](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). If aggregators were made the judges of timeliness, a censoring one could cut a validator's yield without leaving its vote out ([ethresear.ch 29](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)).

### Byzantine tolerance

`human-inspired` Against Byzantine aggregators, alone or with the network adversary. Yann named the Byzantine aggregator; the property against it is the agent's.

- **Definition.** The per-vote and per-round loss probabilities when a share β of stake is Byzantine.
- **The worst a Byzantine aggregator does to liveness is to stay silent,** given safety and no displacement. A vote is then lost when all k aggregators of its subnet are Byzantine: β^k per vote, as for censoring, and the same expected loss under every assignment with replication k (proved).
- **With the network adversary,** each Byzantine aggregator of a subnet saves it one machine: a subnet with j of them costs k − j.
- **Silence is the worst only if nothing waits on a Byzantine aggregator.** `external` One that equivocates decides which of its proofs each node keeps ([ethresear.ch 24](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)), and one that announces a proof and withholds it can keep honest aggregators silent ([ethresear.ch 6](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)).
- **Target.** `human` (Yann's requirement) "Honest-vote delivery should survive at least the finality gadget's own Byzantine tolerance" ([requirements](../design/requirements.md#load)), which is a third of stake.
- **Where we are.** With the `pq` spec's draw of 4 per subnet, withholding alone halts finality at β ≥ 32.6 %, against 33.3 % for the gadget ([aggregator selection](../research/pq-gasper/aggregator-selection.md)).

### Incentive compatibility

`human-inspired` Against rational aggregators. Yann named the rational aggregator; the property against it is the agent's.

- **Definition.** Doing the three steps is each aggregator's best choice when the others do them. It also has to hold for an operator with several roles, and for one that proposes as well.
- **Deviations it has to rule out.** Skipping the proof, since one proof per subnet is enough and k aggregators make one. Proving only the votes that pay, such as one's own. Publishing late to gain. Showing the proof to one proposer only.
- **Replication does not help.** If skipping the proof pays for one aggregator of a subnet, it pays for all k.
- **Where we are.** Open. The [incentives proposal](../design/incentives.md) pays validators for one vote per epoch and caps a proposer's reward per proposal, which leaves "little gain from aggregating your own votes". It names no reward for proving. Lazy and withholding aggregators are listed as griefing in [Q15](../research/open_questions.md).
- **What exists today is indirect.** `external` An aggregator aggregates so that its own vote gets in ([ethresear.ch 37](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md), [discord F6](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). The spec names the deviation that follows: "The dominant strategy for an individual validator is to always broadcast an aggregate containing their own attestation to the global channel" ([specs 3](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)).
- **What is planned is nothing.** `external` For PQ Gasper: "We have not specified additional rewards for aggregators. I do not believe it will be necessary." Against it, a client developer on the rational operator: "I might not want to get the beefy machines that eat into my budget unless there's a reason to" ([discord F31](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). Calls to pay for aggregation exist, and none is worked out ([ethresear.ch 38](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)).
- **The reference spec pays voters and proposers.** `external` "There are no penalties", and "This upgrade does not yet specify rewards for head votes" ([`beacon-chain.md` L286–L293](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/beacon-chain.md?plain=1#L286-L293)). A proposer is paid for the flags that an included vote sets for the first time. Nothing names an aggregator.
- **EIP-8292 lists it as open.** `external` "there are currently no explicit protocol rewards or penalties for aggregators. Because post-quantum proving is costly, relying solely on implicit incentives is riskier" ([EIP-8292 L143](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/68898156500eea7389cf65d746b64c7dd27e4bc1/EIPS/eip-8292.md?plain=1#L143)).
- **Who bears an aggregator's failure.** `external` Under the rewards proposed for EIP-8453, a vote that is not included in the next slot costs its attester and that slot's proposer a penalty. The aggregator pays nothing ([specs 31](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md), an open pull request).
- **Deviations outside the aggregator.** `external` Attesters delay their votes ([ethresear.ch 29](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). An operator can start from an aggregate of its own validators ([ethresear.ch 22](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)), which the [incentives proposal](../design/incentives.md) is written to discourage. A proposer that may aggregate as a last resort must not gain from bypassing the aggregators ([discord F20](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).

### Fair to proposers

`human` At the first level of delivery. Yann, 2026-11-25: "the proposer properties should include fairness (even a low bandwidth proposer should ideally get to include a good aggregate)".

- **Definition.** Every proposer can include a good aggregate, also one on a slow link.
- **It can be relaxed by relying on builders.** `human` A proposer that cannot fetch or merge the proofs in time takes the aggregate from a builder (Yann; [builders note](../posts/pq-gasper-builders.md); [properties](README.md), "Enables aggregation builders").
- **Related.** "Fair rewards for proposers" in the [list of properties](README.md): a home staker's proposal should earn about as much as a big operator's.
- **Where we are.** Today every node is on the one aggregate topic, and making an aggregate takes no proof. For PQ Gasper the [builders note](../posts/pq-gasper-builders.md) has it that, by current simulations, the aggregation topology "seems not performant enough to serve EIP-7870 nodes", and that "Proposers on EIP-7870 nodes rely on builders".
- **Being on the topic is not holding the aggregate.** `external` It is so today ([specs 7](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). Under PQ a simulated home node outside the backbone had 99 % of the aggregates only at 11.17 s ([discord F24](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)), and with few aggregators a proposer can starve: none of its six neighbours aggregated in 5 of 28 simulated networks ([ethresear.ch 7](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md), for a transaction mempool).
- **A path that needs no topic.** `external` The proposer fetches the aggregate over a direct connection, with a bid or on request, with "response size limits and a timeout and local aggregate as fallback" ([discord F30](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).

## Recoverable

`agent` Liveness at the later deadline of the first level: a vote that its aggregators missed can still be proven later, by someone else. It strengthens every liveness property above, because the adversary has to hold out until the round ends. The frame is the agent's; its parts are tagged.

- **Late inclusion.** `human` Late aggregates stay includable until the round ends ([properties](README.md), "Uses the whole round").
  - `external` Reference spec: the window is not the round. A vote can be included while its target is the chain's current or previous target ([Delivered](#votes-and-time), first level), and it is the only copy, because its validator does not vote again at that height ([Round](#votes-and-time)). So late inclusion is the one way a missed vote still counts (the agent's reading). Not upstream as far as the window goes: it is another one there ([Delivered](#votes-and-time), first level). The conclusion holds there too, since a validator votes once per height on both sides.
  - `external` Today the finality part of a vote is paid whenever it lands: "We want validators to be rewarded for correct FFG vote no matter when it makes it on chain" ([specs 16](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). The rewards proposed for EIP-8453 go the other way: full pay in the next slot and none after 16 slots ([specs 31](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)).
  - `external` It needs room on chain: "every missed slot will double the aggregates that the next proposer needs to include" ([discord F32](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)), and almost half of the votes included two slots late were on chain already ([ethresear.ch 30](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). It also needs the caches of [Sybil resilience](#safety) to last as long as the window.
  - `external` One proposed rule for the chain: every included aggregate has to "point to either the previous attestation for the same block, or the block itself, and include it in its STARK merge" ([ethresear.ch 18](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). A late aggregate then costs one more merge, and the proposer has a proving duty of its own.
- **Fallback.** `agent` A proof is valid whoever made it. The `pq` spec at `ee867e9` rejects aggregates from non-members ([design note](../research/pq-gasper/design.md)); a permissionless fallback is mitigation 1 in [aggregator selection](../research/pq-gasper/aggregator-selection.md).
  - `external` Today's stack has this fallback, and a PQ stack loses it unless it adds one: with BLS any proposer aggregates the raw votes its node holds, for free ([specs 17](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md), [ethresear.ch 23](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). Under PQ it takes the raw signatures and a prover.
  - `external` What its absence looks like: on a testnet in 2020, "If we don't get an aggregate then we don't attempt to produce one from naive aggregation pool", and votes that nodes held were lost ([discord F9](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).
  - `external` A cheap form: take a larger proof from elsewhere and narrow it to the claims one holds, in about 0.2 s instead of a new proof ([discord F29](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md), [discord F22](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).
- **What it takes.** A fallback or a later window is more coverage: a vote is lost only if it is lost to the assigned aggregators and to the fallback, and adding a fallback never lowers a tolerance (both proved). With acceptance gated to assigned aggregators a fallback covers nothing, so nothing is recoverable whatever the network does.
- **It pulls against Sybil resilience.** `human-inspired` (Yann: "if everyone can permissionlessly become an aggregator that wouldn't work") A fallback open to everyone does not work: whoever may publish a proof may also flood the topic. The [builders note](../posts/pq-gasper-builders.md) keeps the senders few and staked, the proposers of the last k slots. Whether the third level of delivery can carry a fallback, under the mempool's or a blob's own admission rules, is open (the agent's question).
  - `external` On the agent's question: a blob has an admission rule of its own once blob streaming exists, the ticket ([Sybil resilience](#safety)). A ticket is for one exact slot and is bought a lookahead before it, so a prover that steps in after a failure needs one it bought in advance: "A ticket is valid only during that exact `target_slot`" ([EIP-8256 L235](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/bdc1d099649eefb8790b76f912e25487757f5f4e/EIPS/eip-8256.md?plain=1#L235), a draft; the consequence is the agent's).
- **The last level as a public fallback.** `human` (Yann, 2026-11-25) The last merge, whose proof a proposer includes, can be open to more than the assigned mergers. Who runs it stays in the design space: a sample selected to aggregate, dedicated machines, the proposers of the last k slots ([builders note](../posts/pq-gasper-builders.md)), volunteers, or anyone. The wider the set, the more the point above applies.
  - `external` The same options were laid out on the Discord: the proposer only; the proposer and sampled validators; those and builders; anyone. With two verdicts: "it should still be a selected aggregator, not a volunteer, otherwise it's gonna be a DoS vector", and on builders "I could easily Sybil the builder registry" ([discord F30](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). About ten sampled aggregators per slot were thought to be enough.
  - `external` A precedent for the narrowest set: the payload attestations of Gloas, a vote of 512 members, have no aggregators. "The proposer MUST aggregate all payload attestations with the same data", and the senders are the committee's members ([specs 22](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)).
- **Tolerates missed proposals.** `external` "We want to finalize even if 80% of blocks are missing, so I'd say that we need to be able to aggregate all attestations for the last 5 blocks" ([discord F32](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). Counted in slots of backlog, five slots of 16 proofs are 80 children against a fan-in of 16. Kept by Yann on 2026-11-26.
- **Degrades gracefully.** `external` Work that is half done still counts: "designing the system to be able to degrade gracefully is more important I think than any one fixed hardware requirement" ([discord F38](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). An aggregator proves what it can by the deadline, and a proof of part of a subnet is worth including. The [list of properties](README.md) asks the same of the network as a whole. Kept by Yann on 2026-11-26.

## Performant

`human-inspired` Yann's first note on this line of properties asked for fault tolerance "(and performance)", and on 2026-11-25 he named two of its parts: "There are also CPU load properties, and bandwidth properties." What each requires is the agent's.

- **Latency.** `agent` Time from the vote deadline to a proof that a proposer can include. It has to fit the aggregation phase: 4 s in [Q17](../research/fg/pipelined-units/README.md), 2 s in [Q19](../research/fg/free-running-committees/README.md).
  - `external` Machines that stake are slower than the benchmark machines: 1.8 to 3.6 s to prove 1,400 signatures and 2.2 to 4 s to merge 16 proofs on a current mini PC, "and it's taking 4 seconds with 4 dedicated cores"; 8 to 10 s on an older one ([discord F28](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).
  - `external` EIP-8292 gives the time to prove one aggregate of 1,000 signatures as about 11.0 s on 2 cores, 3.7 s on 8 and 2.6 s on 16 ([EIP-8292 L90–L92](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/68898156500eea7389cf65d746b64c7dd27e4bc1/EIPS/eip-8292.md?plain=1#L90-L92)), and says what a late one is worth: "an aggregate produced too late for the proposer to include provides no value" ([EIP-8292 L50](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/68898156500eea7389cf65d746b64c7dd27e4bc1/EIPS/eip-8292.md?plain=1#L50)).
  - `external` A deadline needs its prover named, and a rule for the node that is slower ([ethresear.ch 8](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). Every merge level is also a round of communication ([ethresear.ch 21](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md), [ethresear.ch 13](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)).
- **Bandwidth.** `human-inspired` Aggregate bytes per slot, for each kind of node: on the topics that every node joins, within the EIP-7870 budget ([Q20](../research/open_questions.md)), and on the topics that only aggregators, mergers and proposers join.
  - `external` Per proof: 200 to 265 KiB for a first proof and 218 to 303 KiB after a merge, by leanVM commit and rate ([ethresear.ch 2](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md), [discord F28](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). Copies: about 4 per proof and 9 per vote in a simulation of 1,000 nodes ([discord F25](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). One copy of 64 proofs needs 17.29 Mbit/s over 6 s ([ethresear.ch 10](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)).
  - `external` The property has to say how proofs travel. Pushing them costs peers × proof size per interval whatever the load. Announcing what a proof covers and fetching on demand follows the load ([ethresear.ch 4](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)), and opens the way to announce and withhold.
  - `external` A floor: one bit per validator has to reach everyone ([ethresear.ch 14](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)).
- **CPU load.** `human-inspired` Proving: signatures per proof, L = d·n, and the hardware it assumes ([Q21](../research/open_questions.md)). Verifying: proofs a node verifies per slot before it forwards them. Merging: the next point.
  - `external` Proving does not stack on one machine: "you still cannot do 16 leaf aggregations in parallel b/c memory bandwidth becomes a bottleneck" ([discord F29](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). Roles on one machine are not only silenced together; they cannot be carried out together either.
- **Merge.** `agent` Proofs per merge, at most m, and the number of merge levels. h levels multiply the votes one proof covers by at most m^h, more when merges add raw signatures (proved). A full merge takes one disjoint cover of the subnets, which is A/k proofs under a redundant assignment and under layers. So the fan-in bounds the aggregators per cover, not the number of subnets.
  - **16 children, 64 subnets.** With aggregators on 4 subnets, one layer is the 16 children of a full merge, as one proof per committee is in the `pq` spec.
  - **After failures the merger mixes layers,** and 16 children that still cover everything exist less often. With 16 of 64 aggregators failed at random: in 27 % of cases under the grid layers and 2 % under shuffled ones, against 96 % under the `pq` layout. Under the grid layers the best 16 children then cover about 62 of the 64 subnets, and covering all that is left takes 16 to 20 ([dispersed note](../research/pq-aggregation/dispersed-assignment/README.md#aggregators-on-a-few-whole-subnets)).
  - **Both ways out stay in the design space** (Yann, 2026-11-25): a second merge level, or 16 children that leave some subnets out. The last level can also be a public fallback ([Recoverable](#recoverable)).
  - `external` Choosing at most m children for the most coverage is the maximum-coverage problem that block packing approximates today ([ethresear.ch 25](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)). Every level also needs its peers found, which was the objection to a second level in 2022 ([discord F16](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).
  - `external` Reference spec: the proposer merges the proofs of up to 16 aggregates of FG attestations and 8 of head votes into the block's one proof ([`validator.md` L480–L486](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/validator.md?plain=1#L480-L486), [`beacon-chain.md` L804–L809](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/beacon-chain.md?plain=1#L804-L809)). That is up to 24 proofs in one merge, where leanVM takes 16 children in a step (the agent's count).
- **Redundant work.** `agent` The share of proving and of aggregate bytes spent on votes that another proof already covers. With replication k, every assignment proves each vote k times at the first level (proved), so (k − 1) / k of that proving is redundant whether or not the assignment is dispersed. Only publishing less saves it. The `pq` spec at `4c29678` staggers the 4 aggregators so that a later one publishes only if no aggregate covers its attesters yet ([design note](../research/pq-gasper/design.md)); that saves the redundant bytes, and the aggregate arrives up to 1.8 s later when the first aggregators fail. Under a dispersed assignment no single earlier proof covers a later aggregator's whole set, so it could stay silent only when several earlier proofs together do, and only if whoever merges combines them.
  - `external` The `pq` spec rules that out as it stands: "Each received aggregate is compared on its own, not together with the others. A proposer can include only one aggregate per committee" ([specs 24](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)).
  - `external` How much silence saves is open. Aggregates of one committee "overlap heavily but rarely cover one another" ([specs 26](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)). The step between two aggregators, 500 to 600 ms, is no longer than packing and sending an aggregate was estimated to take with BLS, 500 to 1,000 ms ([specs 8](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md), [discord F27](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). And the advice of the BLS years was that aggregators "shouldn't wait until other aggregators have sent them aggregates" ([ethresear.ch 25](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)).
  - `external` Measured on mainnet: more than half of the messages on the aggregate topic were duplicates in 2020 ([specs 7](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)), and in 2025 a node received about 6 aggregates per committee, 4 of them distinct ([discord F7](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)).
- **Where we are** ([blob note](../research/pq-aggregation/blob-propagation/README.md); arithmetic on downlink floors, not simulation; examples at 204 KiB per proof).
  - Bytes per node per slot with 64 proofs: 38 to 57 MiB when every node receives every proof whole. 4.8 to 7.2 MiB on topics that only mergers and a subnet's backers join, for a node on 2 subnets of 4 aggregators, and nothing for other nodes. 5.3 MiB for every node when each custodies 8 of 128 columns of every proof.
  - Latency floors: 1.61 to 3.14 s from the vote deadline through one merge of up to 16 proofs, and 2.35 to 4.62 s through two levels over 64. One level fits Q17's 4 s on all four benchmark machines, two levels only on the faster ones.
    - `external` These floors are for the four benchmark machines. On a staking machine that its owner calls top of the line, a merge of 16 alone takes 2.2 to 4 s, and another measurement has 4.48 s on 4 cores next to an execution client ([discord F28](../research/pq-aggregation/background/eth-rnd-discord.md)). With the first proof before it, the first machine needs 4.0 to 7.7 s before any network time (the agent's sum of the two), so one level does not fit Q17's 4 s there.

- **Encoding independent of the topology.** `human` (the 26 Oct breakout) Bitlists and on-chain objects do not depend on who aggregated what ([26 Oct breakout](../meetings/2026-10-26-breakout.md), [Q25](../research/open_questions.md)).
  - `external` Reference spec: committees are fixed by validator index, and an aggregate's bits "are laid out in the committees of the target's epoch, so an aggregate keeps one encoding for as long as it can be included" ([`beacon-chain.md` L279–L281](https://github.com/ethlabs-org/consensus-specs/blob/1e9307bb73a92c7262bc7e571bed1a87570a7832/specs/_features/pq/beacon-chain.md?plain=1#L279-L281)). The encoding names committees, and nothing in it says who aggregated them. Not upstream: there the committees are an even split rotated by epoch, and an aggregate's bits are laid out in the committees of the epoch of its round ([upstream `beacon-chain.md` L2870–L2878](https://github.com/ethereum/consensus-specs/blob/bde2349e1e86763aed845e8a064cb22805628e0c/specs/_features/pq/beacon-chain.md?plain=1#L2870-L2878), the agent's reading). The encoding names committees there too.
  - `external` It has a precedent and a case. EIP-7549 took the committee index out of the signed data. Having it there was, to a reviewer, "a flawed design in phase0" ([specs 19](../research/pq-aggregation/background/consensus-specs.md)).   - `external` One proposal goes the other way and fixes a canonical cover and merge tree, to shrink what an aggregate has to say ([ethresear.ch 14](../research/pq-aggregation/background/ethresearch.md)).
